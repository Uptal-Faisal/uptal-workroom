Implementation Guide

Refund Policy, September 2026 rewrite

Everything the web team needs to ship the new Refund Policy in English and Arabic, plus the Terms & Conditions changes that must go out in the same release.

Prepared: September 7, 2026 Replaces: the policy last updated July 30, 2026 Sections: 13 becomes 19

1 What changed, and why

In plain terms: the new rule is that a successful charge is itself the delivery. The moment the money goes through, on a first purchase or on any renewal, the service for that period is delivered, performed, accepted and earned, and it is not refundable in any form.

The previous policy said services were consumed "immediately upon purchase" but never said that the charge itself constituted delivery, and it carried the renewal rule in only one of the three product sections. It also contained a clause, the CV Enhancer score improvement guarantee, that promised a refund, which contradicted the rest of the document.

1.1 The five substantive changes

  • A charge is a delivery is now its own section and is carried identically through every product, every renewal, every payment method and every price.
  • The CV Enhancer score improvement guarantee is removed. Revisions are the sole remedy. Orders placed before 7 September 2026 keep the guarantee they were sold under, see Section 9.7.
  • Cancellation is self service only. It is done by the subscriber in their account settings. No Uptal employee may cancel for a customer, and a support ticket is expressly not a cancellation.
  • No renewal notices are sent, and the policy says so affirmatively rather than leaving it implied.
  • support@uptal.com is gone. Every contact route is now the ticket form at https://uptal.com/contact-us, and only a ticket counts as notice.

1.2 How the length is handled

The document is long because every clause closes a specific hole that an adversarial pass found. To keep it readable, each section opens with a bolded plain language statement, rendered as a green callout. There are 42 of them and together they state the entire policy in about 1,900 words. A reader can take the whole document from the green boxes alone. This is deliberate: it answers the "buried terms" objection by design rather than by deleting protection.

3 How to implement

The policy lives in an Angular component template. The markup in dropin/ uses the class names the component already styles, so no CSS changes are required.

  1. Replace the contents of <div class="hero"> and the following <div class="scope"> with dropin/hero.en.html.
  2. Replace <nav class="toc"> with dropin/toc.en.html. It now has 19 entries instead of 13.
  3. Replace the whole <article>...</article> with dropin/article.en.html.
  4. Repeat for Arabic with the .ar files, inside the dir="rtl" lang="ar" branch.
  5. Apply source/terms_patch_en.md and source/terms_patch_ar.md to the Terms & Conditions in the same release. This is not optional, see Section 5.
The stylesheet uses logical properties throughout (padding-inline-start, inset-inline-start), so the Arabic renders correctly from the dir="rtl" attribute alone with no separate RTL rules.

5 Terms & Conditions, same release

In plain terms: if the Terms are not patched in the same release, the deleted CV Enhancer guarantee stays legally alive by incorporation, and the price change wording in the Terms reopens the renewal exposure the new policy closes.

Nine amendments, eleven discrete edits, since Amendments 7 and 9 each contain two sub-edits and there is a release edit to the closing date line. They are set out in full in source/terms_patch_en.md and source/terms_patch_ar.md. Every CURRENT block is quoted verbatim from the live page in both languages, so each amendment is a direct find and replace. The retired support address appears inside CURRENT blocks only, because those are the search strings; it appears in no replacement block and is never published. Four of the nine are not cosmetic:

  • Terms 7.7 still references the score improvement guarantee. The Terms are the senior instrument, so leaving this in keeps the guarantee in force even though it has been deleted from the policy.
  • Terms 6.3 says a renewal is charged at "the then-current price for your plan as published on the Website at the time of renewal". That is precisely the wording that lets a charge exceed the amount the customer authorised, which is the trigger for a SAMA Article 90 claim. It must be replaced with the last authorised amount rule.

6 Blocking items before publication

The policy asserts these as facts. If the product does not match, the clause becomes an admission rather than a defence.

Blocker 1. verify the cancellation path word for word on a live paid account of each product. The policy names 'Account, then Billing, then Cancel subscription' in Sections 1.1, 4.3, 7.4, 8.3 and 17.4. If the real path differs by one word, change the product or change all five places. A wrongly described procedure is worse than none: it destroys the disclosed-cancellation-procedure defence in every chargeback and is a consumer protection finding on its own.
Blocker 2. the billing scheduler must not Initiate a Renewal Charge before the Renewal Date and time shown in the subscriber's account. Sections 2.1 and 4.4 now state this as fact. Any early initiation makes the published deadline false.
Blocker 3. checkout must display the exact recurring amount AND the interval, capture an affirmative acceptance, and retain the artefact with timestamp, IP, policy version and language. This is the whole SAMA Article 90 defence now that renewal notices are off the table, and Section 4.2 asserts it as fact.
Blocker 4. obtain the exact statement descriptor string registered with the acquirer and add it to Section 13.6 by amendment. The bracketed pre-publication note has been DELETED and 13.6 now describes the descriptor functionally, so the policy is publishable as it stands, but a Latin string a Saudi cardholder recognises (the merchant of record's legal name is Arabic) must be registered, inserted, and reproduced identically in the purchase confirmation and in the account.
Blocker 5. insert the commercial registration number, national address, VAT registration number and Maroof authentication details into the Uptal electronic store. Section 17.1 states that they are published there, which Article 6 of the E-Commerce Law and Article 6 of the Implementing Regulations require. No placeholder was put in the policy text.
Blocker 6. checkout must display VAT-inclusive totals. Section 13.1 now states that every price displayed before payment is the total price inclusive of VAT and all other amounts. The live Talent Search and Auto-Apply pages currently say 'All prices exclude VAT' and must show the inclusive total alongside any exclusive figure.
Blocker 7. account recovery and password reset must reliably work. With self-service as the sole cancellation route and no ticket fallback (founder decision), a locked out subscriber has no other way to stop billing. Section 4.5 now signposts recovery and reset, so the facility must exist and work.

9 Decisions for Faisal

In plain terms: the adversarial passes added a small number of narrow routes where money goes back to a customer. Each is written so that it is expressly not a refund, using the same architecture as the duplicate charge correction. Each needs a yes or a no.

The recommendation on the money routes is to accept them. Each is either compelled by Saudi law or costs less than the dispute it prevents, and each is drafted so it cannot be used as a precedent.

  1. Decision 1. Card scheme pre-renewal reminders on Talent Search plans billed at six months or more. R10 says no renewal notices to anyone. Section 4.3 now carves out 'any notice that an applicable card scheme rule requires for the billing interval concerned', which sends nothing to any monthly subscriber but stops the policy publishing a statement that Uptal will not do something Mastercard requires. Confirm this narrow carve-out, or instead restrict Talent Search billing intervals to under six months, which removes the issue entirely.
  2. Decision 2. Section 4.9, the failed cancel control. Where Uptal's own Records (or contemporaneous evidence they do not contradict) show the cancel control failed, a Renewal Charge Initiated AFTER the first recorded attempt is now treated as a Charge made after the authority was withdrawn and is corrected under Section 10.2(b), with access and the Billing Period ending on the date of the correction. This is money out, so it needs your sign-off. The reasoning: this is Uptal's own system failing rather than the customer failing, the alternative is losing the chargeback plus the dispute fee plus a finding that the safe harbour was illusory, and the exposure is bounded by Uptal's own logs. A Charge made BEFORE the first recorded attempt stays fully earned and final.
  3. Decision 3. Section 15.11, purchases by minors or persons lacking capacity. The policy now returns the amounts charged, expressly framed as the return of money received under a contract that was not binding, not a refund, not an exception to Section 5.1, no precedent. The affected population is tiny, the money cannot lawfully be kept, and refusing produces a fraud-numerator chargeback Uptal loses anyway. Confirm.
  4. Decision 4. Section 4.16, the Article 4 error correction right. Twenty four hours, available only where the checkout did not let the customer correct the error before submission and the customer has not used or benefited from the service (which under Section 3.1 is almost always false the moment the charge lands). It is mandatory law and its absence was a clean regulator finding. Confirm the narrow drafting.
  5. Decision 5. Section 12.6, the notice fallback. Where the ticketing system itself is unavailable, a written notice Uptal in fact receives counts, provided a Ticket follows as soon as the system is available and with the burden on the customer. Cancellation is expressly excluded from this fallback, so R8 and R9.3 are untouched. This exists because a single privately controlled notice channel with no alternative is exactly what a court reads down. Confirm.
  6. Decision 6. Section 5.9, the liability cap. Raised from 'the amount of that Charge' to the aggregate charged in the preceding twelve months, excluding a liability to return money received without lawful cause. A cap set at exactly the amount in dispute makes the remedy illusory, which is a recognised arbitrariness marker, and it was circular against the SAR 100 cap in the live Terms. The Terms cap must be raised in the same release.
  7. Decision 7. Are Auto-Apply and CV Enhancer sold to non-Saudi issued cards? If yes, either restrict acceptance to KSA issued cards at the gateway or accept and budget the exposure. Section 1.11 governs the contract but cannot bind a foreign issuer applying its own rules, and in some markets an unenforceable exclusion of statutory withdrawal rights is itself a regulatory finding.
  8. Decision 8. The exact statement descriptor string for Section 13.6, and the commercial registration number, national address, VAT registration number and Maroof store number for Section 17.1. None was inserted, because a bracketed placeholder in a published legal instrument is worse than the gap.
  9. Decision 9. Whether to keep the ARCHIVED ALTERNATIVE BLOCK at the end of final_en.md in the file you hand to the web team. It is clearly labelled as not for publication and must be stripped before the page goes live.
