## Method

Both files were read in full, side by side. The two documents turned out to be written paragraph for paragraph on the same line numbers, so parity could be tested mechanically as well as by reading. The following were compared line by line across the whole pair:

- every heading, its level (`##` / `###` / `####`) and its number;
- every blank line, horizontal rule, bullet marker and bold-run marker;
- every cross reference token (`\d+\.\d+` plus sub-paragraph letter), with Arabic letters normalised — (أ)→(a), (ب)→(b), (هـ)→(e);
- every parenthesised numeral (periods, thresholds, statute article and paragraph numbers);
- the day-type attached to every period (calendar day vs Business Day);
- every occurrence of the ticketing URL, the privacy policy URL, the "Billing & payments" / "الفوترة والمدفوعات" reason code and the date 7 September 2026;
- enumeration length inside single-paragraph lists, by semicolon count.

House-style rules from the standing legal suite were also checked on the Arabic: no em dashes or en dashes (0 of each), «الكفاءات» never «المواهب» (17 / 0), no «سوق الوظايف», no trailing whitespace, no double spaces.

Three renderings differ in wording. None is a gap; all three are recorded here so the position is on the record.

**(a) §1.1 summary box, sixth bullet — the Arabic is the accurate one.**
English: "or a charge was made **without the cardholder's authority**".
Arabic: «أو تم خصم مبلغ **دون تفويض من الدافع المسجَّل**» (without the authority of the Payer of Record).
The operative provision, §10.2(b), turns on the **Payer of Record** in both languages, and §2.1 defines Payer of Record as the holder of the payment method as identified in the Records "or, where that person is not identifiable, the Account Holder" — which is not the same class as "cardholder". The Arabic summary therefore matches the operative rule and the English summary does not. Because the Arabic prevails, no operative gap arises. The English should still be corrected: see Part 5.

**(b) §15.5, second paragraph — the Arabic is marginally broader, in Uptal's favour, and deliberately so.**
English closes: "...or gives rise to any right, entitlement, expectation, **estoppel**, or representation."
Arabic closes: «...ولا ينشئ أي حق أو استحقاق أو توقع أو إقرار أو تعهد، **ولا يمنع شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات من التمسك بأي حكم من أحكام هذه السياسة**.»
Estoppel has no clean civil-law counterpart. Rendering it as an express non-preclusion clause states the intended effect in terms a Saudi court can apply, where the literal «منع من التناقض» would state less. The Arabic is not narrower anywhere; it is slightly wider and in the same direction as the English. Left as drafted.

**(c) §2.2 and §19 final paragraph — necessary localisation of "capitalised terms".**
English: "Capitalised terms used but not defined in this Policy bear the meanings given to them in the Terms & Conditions."
Arabic: defined terms are identified by quotation marks instead, and §2.2 adds the enabling sentence «وتُميَّز المصطلحات المعرَّفة في هذه السياسة بإيرادها بين علامتَي تنصيص عند تعريفها؛ وتحمل المصطلحات المميَّزة على هذا النحو والمستخدمة في هذه السياسة دون أن تكون معرَّفة فيها المعاني المبيَّنة لها في الشروط والأحكام.»
Arabic script has no capitalisation, so a literal translation would have left the incorporation mechanism inoperative. The added sentence is required to make the identical rule work and is not surplus content. §19 carries the matching localisation. Correct as drafted.

## 5. Defects found and what was done

### Fixed in `final_ar.md` (3)

**A1 — §11.5, line 880. Grammatical defect in an operative cross reference. Fixed.**
Was: «...ولا ينشئ أي حق أو استحقاق أو توقّع أو سابقة أو عرف أو مجرى تعامل، وفقًا **للقسمان** 5.7 و10.9.»
Now: «...وفقًا **للقسمين** 5.7 و10.9.»
A dual noun governed by the preposition لـ takes the genitive (القسمين), not the nominative (القسمان). This was the only such error in the file; the other 15 dual-section references are all correctly inflected — القسمين after a preposition (§3.1, §4.10, §4.15, §5.3, §6.9, §11.1, §17.3 and the archive's consequential-amendment list) and القسمان where the dual is the grammatical subject or a citation label (§3.5, §4 introduction, §4.10, §14.2, §14.3, archive §10.5, archive amendment label).

**A2 — §9.7, line 743. Repetition defect. Fixed.**
Was: «...بنسخة هذه السياسة المنشورة وقت تقديم ذلك الطلب، بما في ذلك **ذلك** الضمان والشروط التي كان مقيَّدًا بها.»
Now: «...بنسخة هذه السياسة المنشورة وقت تقديم ذلك الطلب، **بما فيها** ذلك الضمان والشروط التي كان مقيَّدًا بها.»
The old text stacked the fixed phrase «بما في ذلك» against the demonstrative «ذلك», producing a ذلك/ذلك stutter in the sentence that preserves the former score improvement guarantee. The replacement attaches the pronoun to «نسخة هذه السياسة», which is the intended antecedent, and keeps «ذلك الضمان» = "that guarantee" exactly. No change of meaning or scope.

**A3 — archive boundary, line 1234. Structural parity. Fixed.**
The English marks the break before the archived block with two consecutive horizontal rules (`---` / `---`); the Arabic had one. This pushed the entire archived block one line out of alignment and was the only structural divergence in the pair. A second `---` was added. Both files are now 1,290 lines with exact 1:1 line correspondence end to end, which is the invariant §19 asserts and the thing that makes mechanical parity checking possible on every future revision.
