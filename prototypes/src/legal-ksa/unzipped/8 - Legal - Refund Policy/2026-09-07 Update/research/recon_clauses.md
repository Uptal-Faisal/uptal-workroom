# 1. Deemed delivery and deemed acceptance

**What the discipline is.** A plain "payments are non-refundable" sentence is a promise about a
remedy. It invites the counter argument "but you did not deliver, so there is nothing to be
non-refundable about." Disciplined drafters never fight on the remedy. They fight on **performance**:
they define the moment of performance, define the measure of performance, and then deem both to have
occurred at an instant that the merchant controls and can prove. Once delivery and acceptance are
complete, the refund question never arises, because there is no failure of consideration to complain
about. The live policy is already halfway there (Section 2 is genuinely good), but it says the service
is "treated as used", which is an inference. The new clause must say it is **delivered, performed and
accepted**, which is a legal conclusion.

**Four things the clause must do, in this order:**
1. Fix the moment (successful charge, defined precisely, not settlement).
2. Deem delivery complete for the whole period at that moment.
3. Fix the measure of performance as availability, and expressly disclaim usage as the measure.
4. Deem acceptance at the same instant, with no acceptance period and no right of rejection.

Then two supports: the entirety of the period as an indivisible unit, and the characterisation of the
money as earned income rather than a prepayment.

## DRAFT 1.1 The charge is the delivery

> ### 3.1 Charge equals delivery
>
> **In plain terms: the moment your payment goes through, the service for that period has been
> delivered to you in full. That is true of your first payment and of every renewal.**
>
> At the moment a payment is Successfully Charged in respect of any Uptal product, plan, order,

> item, the service to which that charge relates is, for that Billing Period, delivered in full,
> performed in full, accepted in full, and irrevocably consumed, and the amount charged is at that same
> moment earned in full by Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) and becomes final and
> non-refundable in accordance with this Policy. This Section applies identically, and without
> distinction of any kind, to an initial or first purchase and to every subsequent renewal and every
> subsequent charge, on every payment method, in every currency, on every plan, and for every product,
> and no different or more favourable treatment applies to any charge by reason of its being the first,
> the most recent, the largest, the smallest, an automatic charge, a retried charge, a discounted or
> promotional charge, or a charge made on a payment method that you did not personally re-enter at the
> time of the charge.
>
> For the purposes of this Policy, a payment is **Successfully Charged** at the moment the relevant
> transaction is authorized and accepted by Uptal's payment processor and recorded as successful in
> Uptal's billing records, irrespective of the time at which the amount is subsequently settled to
> Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات), of the time at which it appears on your statement, of
> any subsequent action by you, your bank, or your card issuer, and of whether or not you open, log in

## DRAFT 1.2 Availability, not usage, is the measure of performance

The live policy already contains the best sentence in the whole document at Section 3.1: "it being the
availability of the service, and not your actual usage of it, that constitutes performance for the
purposes of this Policy." That sentence is currently buried inside the Talent Search section and
therefore, on a strict reading, applies only to Talent Search. Promote it, generalise it, and reinforce
it.

> ### 3.2 Availability is performance
>
> **In plain terms: you are paying for the service to be there for you, not for the amount you use it.**

## DRAFT 1.4 The Billing Period is indivisible, and the fee is earned income

These two supports do more work than their length suggests. The first kills every pro rata argument at
source rather than by listing pro rata as a non-ground. The second kills the "you are holding my money
in advance" framing, which is the framing that makes a court reach for restitution.

> ### 3.4 Indivisibility of the Billing Period
>
> Each Billing Period is a single, entire, and indivisible unit of service, and the price for it is a
> single, entire, and indivisible price. The price is not, and shall not be treated as, a daily,
> weekly, monthly, per-application, per-unlock, per-match, per-revision, or otherwise divisible or
> apportionable rate, and no part of it is attributable to any particular day, feature, allowance, or
> unit of output within the Billing Period. Accordingly, no apportionment, proration, division,
> reduction, abatement, or partial calculation of any charge shall be made or claimed for any reason,
> including cancellation, suspension, termination, non-use, downgrade, plan change, pausing, account
> deletion, or any period of unavailability.
>
> ### 3.5 Character of the amounts paid
>
> Each amount Successfully Charged is paid as the price of a service delivered at the moment of charge.
> It is not, and shall not at any time be construed as, a deposit, an advance, a prepayment, a payment
> on account, a security, an escrow, a float, a stored value, a wallet balance, a credit against future
> services, or any sum held to your order or on your behalf. Title to, and full beneficial ownership
> of, each such amount passes to Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) at the moment of charge,
> and Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) holds no amount on trust, on deposit, or as agent
> for you at any time.

## DRAFT 1.5 The immediate performance consent, restated as a standing consent

The live Section 2 already has this and it must be preserved (R7). Two upgrades: make the consent
**standing and forward looking** so it covers every future renewal, and add the express acknowledgement
of the consequence, which is the part that actually engages the digital content exception.

> ### 3.6 Your request for immediate performance and its consequences
>
> By completing any purchase, and by maintaining any subscription in effect, you expressly request, and
> expressly consent to, Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) commencing and completing
> performance of the relevant service immediately upon each charge, before the expiry of any
> withdrawal, cancellation, cooling-off, or similar period that might otherwise apply. This request and
> consent are given once, at the time of your first purchase or subscription, and are standing,
> continuing, and irrevocable in respect of every subsequent charge and every subsequent Billing
> Period, and are not required to be, and will not be, sought again at any renewal or subsequent
> charge. You acknowledge and agree that, performance having been requested by you and commenced and
> completed accordingly, any statutory right of withdrawal, return, or cancellation you might otherwise
> have had in respect of that charge is thereby affected, reduced, or extinguished, in each case to the
> maximum extent permitted by applicable Saudi law, and you acknowledge that this consequence has been
> disclosed to you before payment.

## DRAFT 1.6 The cascade, for the clause that gets read down

This is what separates a policy written for volume from a policy written for survival. Assume a
tribunal refuses to accept availability alone as performance. Do not let that refusal collapse the
whole clause; give it somewhere softer to land that still produces no refund.

> ### 3.7 Alternative bases
>
> The bases on which each charge is final and non-refundable are separate, independent, and cumulative,
> and each is intended to apply on its own. If, in any case or in respect of any person, a tribunal of
> competent jurisdiction determines that availability alone does not constitute complete performance,
> then each charge shall nonetheless be final and non-refundable on the following further and
> independent bases, each of which applies to the maximum extent permitted by applicable Saudi law:
> that performance in fact commenced immediately upon the charge and continued throughout the Billing
> Period; that access and entitlements were in fact granted, provisioned, and made operative in your
> account, as evidenced by the Records; that you expressly requested immediate performance under
> Section 3.6; that the service is a digital service supplied otherwise than on a tangible medium, the
> supply of which began with your prior express consent; and that any remedy otherwise available is
> limited to the minimum required by that law and is reduced by the value of the access, availability,
> and performance in fact provided to you before the claim arose.

**Notes / survivability.**
- The Saudi E-Commerce Law withdrawal exception for digital content is, in practice, evidenced by
  **access**. Advisory commentary is consistent that operators relying on a blanket digital no-return
  position are exposed precisely where the consumer can show the content was never accessed. That is
  why 1.6 exists, why part 4 (records) is not optional, and why the product side must actually log
  provisioning at the instant of charge, not at first login.
- Do not write "conclusively deemed delivered for all purposes including under mandatory law". Deeming
  provisions that purport to bind a court on a question of fact reserved to it are the classic thing
  that gets struck. "Deemed, to the maximum extent permitted by applicable Saudi law" survives.
- Keep the plain English lead sentences in bold. The live policy already uses plain leads, and a policy
  that a consumer can understand is materially harder to attack as an unfair surprise term.

# 2. Renewal parity

**What the discipline is.** The single largest source of subscription refund pressure is not the first
purchase, it is the renewal, and the arguments are always the same three: "I forgot", "I cancelled
immediately after", and "I did not know it was recurring". Each of these is an attack on **consent at
the moment of the renewal charge**. The answer is not to argue about the customer's state of mind. It
is to establish that the renewal is a fresh purchase made under authority that was validly granted
once, in advance, for all future occasions, and that the customer held, at all times, an unconditional
self-serve power to stop it.

## DRAFT 2.1 Each renewal is a new purchase

> ### 4.1 Every renewal is a new purchase, fully performed
>
> **In plain terms: a renewal is not a continuation of an old purchase. It is a new purchase, and it is
> delivered the moment it is charged, exactly like your first one.**
>
> Each renewal of a subscription constitutes a separate and distinct purchase of a new Billing Period,
> on the terms of this Policy and the Terms & Conditions as in force at the time of that renewal, and
> at the then-current price for your plan. Each renewal charge is subject to Section 3 in the same way,
> and to the same extent, as an initial purchase: at the moment the renewal payment is Successfully
> Charged, the service for the renewed Billing Period is delivered in full, performed in full, accepted
> in full, and irrevocably consumed, and the amount charged is earned in full and becomes final and
> non-refundable. No renewal charge is provisional, conditional, revocable, or subject to any period of
> reflection, reconsideration, or confirmation, and the length of time for which you have held the
> subscription, the number of Billing Periods for which you have previously been charged, and the
> extent to which you used any previous Billing Period are irrelevant to the finality of any renewal
> charge.

## DRAFT 2.2 Advance consent to immediate performance on every renewal

> ### 4.2 Standing authority and advance consent
>
> By subscribing, you authorize Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) and its payment
> processors to charge your selected payment method automatically, without further authorization,
> notice, confirmation, or action by you, on each renewal date, for each successive Billing Period,
> until the subscription is cancelled in accordance with this Policy. That authority, and the request
> and consent to immediate performance given under Section 3.6, are given in advance and once only, at
> the time you subscribe, and extend to every future renewal and to every future Billing Period. They
> also extend to, and remain valid and effective notwithstanding: any change to the price of your plan
> taking effect at renewal; any change to the composition, features, or allowances of the product; the
> replacement, updating, re-issue, or expiry and automatic update of your card or payment credentials,
> whether by you, by your card issuer, or by an automatic account updater service operated by a card
> scheme or payment provider; any change in the identity of Uptal's payment processor; and any period
> of non-use.

## DRAFT 2.3 The three arguments, killed by name

> ### 4.3 Cancellation is your responsibility, and it operates forwards only
>
> Cancellation is available to you at any time, without charge, without justification, and without
> contacting any person, from your Uptal account settings, and additionally by contacting
> support@uptal.com. Your renewal date is shown in your account at all times. The responsibility for
> cancelling before a renewal date rests solely and exclusively with you. Accordingly, and to the
> maximum extent permitted by applicable Saudi law:
>
> - **Forgetting to cancel is not a ground.** Failure or omission to cancel before a renewal date, for
>   any reason, including inadvertence, absence, illness, travel, loss of access to your email account,
>   loss of access to your payment method, or having forgotten that the subscription existed, does not
>   affect the validity or finality of the renewal charge and is not a ground for any refund or other
>   remedy.
> - **Cancelling after the renewal charge is not a ground.** Cancellation takes effect at the end of
>   the Billing Period then current, and has effect in respect of future Billing Periods only. No
>   cancellation, whenever submitted, has retrospective effect, and a cancellation submitted after a
>   renewal charge has been Successfully Charged, including one submitted in the same minute, the same
>   hour, or the same day as that charge, does not cancel, reverse, void, or entitle you to any
>   repayment of that charge, and instead takes effect at the end of the Billing Period that the charge
>   purchased.
> - **Not having realised the subscription was recurring is not a ground.** The recurring nature of the
>   subscription, the renewal frequency, the amount, and the means of cancellation are disclosed to you
>   before you pay, are recorded in the confirmation of your purchase, and are available in your
>   account at all times. Any reminder, renewal notice, receipt, or other communication that Uptal
>   (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) may send in relation to a renewal is sent as a courtesy
>   only; it is not a condition of the renewal, does not form part of the authority granted under
>   Section 4.2, and neither the non-sending of, nor your non-receipt of, non-opening of, or failure to
>   read, any such communication, for any reason including email filtering, an out of date email
>   address, or a full mailbox, affects the validity or finality of any renewal charge.
> - **Dormancy is not cancellation.** A period of non-use, however long, does not cancel, pause,
>   suspend, or terminate a subscription, and Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) is under no
>   obligation to monitor your usage, to notify you that you are not using a product, or to cancel a
>   subscription on your behalf.

## DRAFT 2.4 Plan changes, reactivations, add-ons and retried charges

> ### 4.4 Upgrades, downgrades, plan changes, reactivations and add-ons
>
> Every upgrade, downgrade, plan change, reactivation of a cancelled or lapsed subscription, additional
> seat, add-on, or supplementary purchase is a separate purchase to which Sections 3 and 4.1 apply in
> full. In particular: an upgrade charge is final and non-refundable from the moment it is Successfully
> Charged; a downgrade takes effect at the end of the Billing Period then current and does not entitle
> you to any repayment, apportionment, or credit in respect of the difference in price for the Billing
> Period then current or for any earlier Billing Period; a reactivation is a new purchase at the
> then-current price, and no allowance, entitlement, balance, or unexpired portion of any earlier

> and subsequently retried, whether automatically or at your request, is a single charge for that
> Billing Period and is final once Successfully Charged.

## DRAFT 2.5 Pausing

Preserve the live Auto-Apply pause (4.4) but bolt it to the core so it cannot be read as a partial
refund mechanism.

> ### 4.5 Pausing
>
> Where a pause facility is offered for a product, pausing suspends future charges and future
> performance from the end of the Billing Period then current. Pausing has no effect on any charge
> already Successfully Charged, does not shorten, extend, suspend, or carry forward the Billing Period
> then current, and does not constitute, and shall not be construed as, a refund, credit, extension, or
> deferral of any amount already paid.

**Notes / survivability.**
- Saudi practice expects auto-renewal to be clearly disclosed and cancellation to be easy, including
  one-click style self-service. Clause 4.3 asserts exactly that as a fact. **It must be true.** If the
  cancellation control is buried, or if cancellation requires emailing support, then 4.3 is a
  misstatement of fact in a published policy, which is worse than not having the clause. Verify the
  product before publishing this wording.
- Do not draft "you waive any right to be notified of a renewal". Draft what is above: the notice is a
  courtesy and non-receipt does not affect validity. Same outcome, no waiver of a possible statutory
  disclosure duty on the face of the document.

# 3. The exhaustive "no refund in any form" enumeration

**What the discipline is.** "No refunds" only closes the door marked refund. Every experienced claimant
walks around to a side door: a credit, an extension, a goodwill gesture, a set-off, damages in lieu.
The enumeration exists so that the claimant cannot say "I am not asking for a refund". It has to be
followed by an **anti-characterisation sweep**, because no list is ever complete, and the sweep is what
makes the list exhaustive in effect rather than in appearance.

## What the brief's list is missing

The brief already names: refund, reversal, credit, voucher, coupon, discount, account balance,
extension, free period, set-off, deduction, withholding, compensation, damages in lieu, goodwill. Add:

**Money and money equivalents:** partial refund; pro rata or apportioned refund; rebate; cashback;
price adjustment or retroactive repricing; price match; refund of the difference on a downgrade or a
subsequent promotion; store credit; gift card; wallet or stored value; loyalty points; service credit
or SLA credit; promotional credit; payment holiday; waiver, forgiveness, or reduction of any future
fee; free or discounted future Billing Period; free upgrade, free add-on, or free plan tier.

**Service in lieu of money:** extension or prolongation of the Billing Period; additional or restored
allowances (profile unlocks, applications, revisions, iterations, seats); rollover or carry forward of
unused allowances; re-performance of the service; a substitute or replacement product; transfer of a
subscription, an unexpired period, or an allowance to another person, account, plan, or product;
reinstatement of a lapsed period.

**Legal characterisations that lawyers reach for when the word refund is closed off:** restitution;
unjust enrichment; failure of consideration; quantum meruit; money had and received; rescission and
repayment; price reduction, abatement, or diminution in price; damages, including damages in lieu of
performance; liquidated damages; indemnity; contribution; recoupment; retention; lien; set-off,
counterclaim, cross-claim, or abatement against any amount due to Uptal.

**Channel sweep:** the same claim advanced through a bank, a card issuer, a payment provider, a
chargeback, a reversal request, a mobile application store, a third party claims service, or any other
route.

# 4. Evidentiary clauses

**What the discipline is.** In every real dispute the fight is factual, not legal: was there a charge,
was access granted, was the work done. Whoever controls the record wins. Serious merchants therefore do
three things: they define their records as **the** record, they place the evidential burden on the
claimant, and they secure in advance the customer's consent to disclose those records to card schemes
and regulators. The third is the one most policies forget, and it is the one that wins chargebacks.

## DRAFT 4.1 Records as the record

> ### 13.1 Uptal's records
>
> Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) maintains records of each charge and of the
> availability, provisioning, and performance of each service (the "**Records**"). The Records include,
> without limitation: payment authorization and settlement records and transaction references received
> from Uptal's payment processors; the date, time, amount, currency, and payment method associated with
> each charge; the plan, product, and Billing Period to which each charge relates; account creation,
> authentication, and session logs, including internet protocol addresses, device and browser
> identifiers, and timestamps; provisioning and entitlement records evidencing that access, features,
> and allowances were granted and made operative; usage logs, including searches, profile unlocks,
> matches, tailored CVs, applications submitted, deliverables issued, and revisions provided; the
> content and timing of checkout screens, disclosures, and consents presented to and accepted by you;
> confirmations, receipts, tax invoices, and renewal communications sent to you and their delivery
> status; and correspondence with you, including support tickets and their timestamps.
>
> ### 13.2 Evidential status
>
> You agree that the Records are the definitive record of each charge and of the availability,
> provisioning, and performance of each service, and that they shall constitute conclusive evidence of
> those matters, save in the case of manifest error, in each case to the maximum extent permitted by
> applicable Saudi law. You further agree that the Records, and copies, extracts, printouts, screen
> captures, and electronic reproductions of them, are admissible in any proceeding, investigation, or
> dispute, and you waive, to the maximum extent permitted by applicable Saudi law, any objection to
> their admission or weight based on their being electronic, being copies rather than originals, being
> generated automatically, or not bearing a handwritten signature.

## DRAFT 4.2 Burden and contemporaneity

The contemporaneous complaint requirement is quietly the most powerful clause in this part. It converts
silence at the time into evidence against the claimant later.

> ### 13.3 Burden of proof and contemporaneous notice
>
> Any person asserting that a charge was not made, that access or availability was not provided, or
> that a service was not performed, bears the burden of establishing that assertion by contemporaneous
> documentary evidence. Where you assert that a product was unavailable, inaccessible, or not
> functioning during any period, you must have reported that matter to support@uptal.com at the time it
> occurred and while it was continuing, so that it could be investigated and, where possible, resolved.
> The absence of a contemporaneous report is evidence that the product was available and functioning
> during the period in question, and a report first made after the end of the relevant Billing Period,
> after a cancellation, or after a payment dispute has been raised, shall be given such weight, if any,
> as the circumstances warrant.

## DRAFT 4.3 Consent to disclose, for dispute defence

> ### 13.4 Use and disclosure of the Records
>
> You consent to Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) compiling, using, and disclosing the
> Records, in whole or in part, to its payment processors, acquiring banks, card issuers, card schemes,
> and other payment providers, to any competent court, authority, or regulator, and to its professional
> advisers, for the purposes of evidencing a charge, defending or responding to a chargeback, payment
> dispute, reversal request, complaint, or claim, and complying with applicable law and card scheme
> rules. Such disclosure is made in accordance with the Privacy Policy and applicable Saudi law,
> including the Personal Data Protection Law.

## DRAFT 4.4 Retention

> ### 13.5 Retention
>
> Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) retains the Records for the period required by
> applicable Saudi law, including the tax and commercial record-keeping requirements of the Kingdom of
> Saudi Arabia, and in any event for so long as is necessary for the purposes described in Section
> 13.4. The expiry of any retention period, and the consequent unavailability of a Record, does not
> create, revive, or support any claim.

**Notes / survivability.**
- **Say "conclusive evidence, save in the case of manifest error".** A bare "conclusive evidence"
  clause purporting to bind the court on a question of fact is the most commonly struck evidential
  clause there is. The manifest error carve-out is the standard formulation in banking documents
  precisely because it survives, and it costs nothing: manifest error means an obvious arithmetical or
  clerical error, not a disagreement.
- The Records list is deliberately written to match what card schemes accept as compelling evidence for
  a digital goods dispute, which turns on proving the cardholder participated in the transaction or
  benefited from it: user identifier, internet protocol address, device identifier, login and session
  history, and evidence of the service being used. Ensure the product actually captures the internet
  protocol address and a device identifier at signup and at each renewal, or the clause describes
  records that do not exist.
- Section 13.4 is also the clause that lets support attach evidence to a representment without a fresh
  data protection analysis each time. It is worth its own paragraph.

# 5. Time bar clauses for billing claims

**What the discipline is.** Two separate bars, drafted separately, because they do different jobs and
have different survival odds. A **notification bar** is a contractual condition precedent: raise it
quickly or the claim is not admissible. A **limitation bar** shortens the period for bringing
proceedings, and is the more vulnerable of the two because mandatory limitation periods generally
cannot be shortened by agreement. Draft both, make each independent, and put an anti-tolling sentence
underneath so that talking to support does not stop the clock.

## DRAFT 5.1

> ### 14.1 Time limits for billing claims
>
> **In plain terms: if you think something is wrong with a charge, tell us quickly. Waiting removes the
> ability to look into it.**
>
> Any claim, query, objection, or dispute of any kind relating to a charge, an invoice, an amount, a
> currency, a tax, or a payment method, including any request made under Section 11 (Correction of
> charges that are not payments for services), must be made by you in writing to support@uptal.com
> within seven (7) calendar days of the date of the charge concerned, or, if later, within seven (7)
> calendar days of the date on which you first became aware, or ought reasonably to have become aware,
> of the matter complained of, and in any event within sixty (60) calendar days of the date of the
> charge. Your notice must state the transaction reference, the amount, the date of the charge, the
> payment method used, and a full description of the circumstances relied upon, and must be accompanied
> by such information, documentation, and cooperation as Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات)
> may reasonably request.
>
> ### 14.2 Effect of the time limits
>
> Compliance with Section 14.1 is a condition precedent to the making of any claim in respect of a
> charge. Each charge, and each tax invoice issued in respect of it, is deemed to be correct, accepted,
> and free from objection, and any claim in respect of it is deemed to be irrevocably waived and
> released, upon the expiry of the applicable period in Section 14.1 without a compliant notice having
> been given, in each case to the maximum extent permitted by applicable Saudi law. Without limiting
> the foregoing, and to the extent permitted by applicable Saudi law, no proceeding in respect of any

> ### 14.3 No extension of time
>
> Time is of the essence in respect of the periods in Section 14.1. Those periods are not suspended,
> extended, tolled, postponed, restarted, or renewed by: any correspondence, discussion, or
> negotiation with Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) or its support team; any investigation
> being conducted; any request for further information; the filing of a chargeback, payment dispute, or
> complaint with any bank, card issuer, payment provider, or third party; the making of a complaint to
> any authority; any period of account suspension; or any absence, illness, or unavailability on your

**Notes / survivability.**
- The "or, if later, within seven days of when you first became aware" limb is not a concession, it is
  armour. A flat seven days from the charge date is very likely to be read down as unreasonable where
  a customer discovers an unauthorised charge on a monthly statement. The layered version gives a
  tribunal a reasonable clause to enforce instead of an unreasonable one to strike, and the sixty day
  longstop keeps the exposure closed.
- Twelve months matches Terms Section 16 exactly. Keep them identical or the inconsistency becomes the
  argument.
- Do not draft a contractual interest charge on late amounts anywhere in this policy. Interest is not
  enforceable in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. Recovery of documented costs, as in part 9, is the
  correct mechanism.

# 6. Severability and blue pencil

**What the discipline is.** Ordinary severability deletes the bad provision. Blue pencil severance
narrows it. What a top tier firm adds beyond both is a **cascade**: an express instruction to the
tribunal on the order in which to read the clause down, and a floor beneath which the merchant still
wins. The point of the cascade is that a tribunal that is unwilling to enforce the clause as drafted is
usually willing to enforce a narrower version, and will take the narrower version you have written
rather than invent one.

## DRAFT 6.1

> ### 15.1 Severability and reading down
>

> effect in every other case; and

> be affected, and this Policy shall continue in full force and effect.
>
> ### 15.2 Order of reading down
>
> Where a provision of this Policy falls to be modified under Section 15.1(a), it shall be read down in
> the following order, and only to the extent necessary at each stage before proceeding to the next:
> first, by limiting its application to those persons in respect of whom it may lawfully apply; second,
> by limiting its application to those products, plans, or transactions in respect of which it may

> inapplicable in respect of any person, product, transaction, circumstance, or jurisdiction: (i) it

**Notes / survivability.**
- 15.3(iii) is the clause that pays for this whole part. Even in the worst case, where a mandatory
  right is found to apply, the exposure is a net figure after value received, not a gross repayment.
- Do not attempt a contra proferentem exclusion clause. Under the Saudi Civil Transactions Law,
  ambiguity in an adhesion contract is construed in favour of the adhering party, and a clause

# 7. No waiver, no course of dealing, ex gratia, anti precedent, variation

**Notes / survivability.**
- Card scheme rules expect a refund and cancellation policy to be **displayed at the point of sale**,

  the checkout state in the Records.

  fastest way to lose a regulator's sympathy on everything else in the document.

# 10. Force majeure, discontinuation and sunset, without a refund entitlement

**What the discipline is.** Two distinct risks are usually conflated. **Force majeure** is a failure to
perform that is excused. **Discontinuation** is a decision by the merchant to stop performing, which is
not excused by anything and is therefore the more dangerous of the two, because a customer who paid for
a year and had the product withdrawn in month two has the most sympathetic story available. The
disciplined answer is to define the subject matter of the purchase as the service **as it exists from
time to time**, so that changing its composition is not a failure of performance at all.

## DRAFT 10.1 Availability and disruption

> ### 10.1 No warranty of availability

> Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) endeavours to maintain continuous availability of its
> services but does not warrant, represent, or undertake that any service will be available,
> uninterrupted, timely, secure, or error-free at any time or for any period. Temporary or extended
> disruptions, degradations, suspensions, and outages may occur, whether by reason of scheduled or
> emergency maintenance, system upgrades, capacity constraints, security measures, changes to or
> failures of third party infrastructure, telecommunications, hosting, payment, or data providers,
> changes to or restrictions imposed by any employer, applicant tracking system, job platform, or other
> third party source, a Force Majeure Event, or any other technical, operational, legal, or external
> cause, whether foreseen or unforeseen.

> ### 10.2 No refund for disruption or Force Majeure

> the extent required by mandatory applicable Saudi law. In this Policy, "**Force Majeure Event**" has
> the meaning given to it in the Terms & Conditions.

> Upon the expiry, cancellation, suspension, or termination of a subscription or of your account, your

> within it ceases at the end of the Billing Period then current or, in the case of termination for

## OPTIONAL 10.4 The sunset substitute, for the founder's decision

This is presented separately because it is a discretion, and the brief's instruction is that
discretions are not to be added. It is included because it is the single cheapest way to make the
discontinuation position look reasonable to a regulator, and it is not a refund, a credit, or an
extension of term.

> Where Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) permanently discontinues a paid product during a
> Billing Period for which you have been Successfully Charged, Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) may, at its sole discretion, make available to you a substitute or successor Uptal product
> for the remainder of that Billing Period. Any such substitution is made at Uptal's discretion, is not
> a refund, credit, or extension, does not entitle you to any choice of product or to any comparison of
> value, and creates no precedent and no entitlement in any other case.

**Recommendation:** include it. It costs nothing (the substitute is an existing product, at zero
marginal cost), it is expressly not a refund, and it removes the only fact pattern in the whole policy
where "you charged me and then took the product away" has real force.

**Notes / survivability.**
- 10.3 is the highest value clause in this part and the one most policies omit. It is what makes a
  product change a non-event rather than a partial failure of consideration.
- Total, prolonged, merchant-caused unavailability of a paid service for an entire Billing Period is
  the one scenario where a Saudi tribunal applying general principles is most likely to intervene
  regardless of drafting, because the customer received nothing at all. That is a genuine residual
  exposure and is listed in the risks. The mitigation is operational, not textual: an incident and
  substitute-access process so the fact pattern never arises.

# Appendix A. Drafting instructions for whoever writes the final text

1. **Put the deeming clause third, not thirteenth.** The core is Sections 3, 4, and 5. Every
   product section (Talent Search, Auto-Apply, CV Enhancer) then reads "Sections 3 to 5 apply in full
   to [product]", followed only by the product-specific non-grounds. This removes three near-duplicate
   no refund sections and leaves one clause to defend instead of four inconsistent ones.
2. **Use one defined term for the trigger and use it everywhere.** "Successfully Charged". Do not let
   "processed", "completed", "taken", "settled", or "made" appear as loose synonyms anywhere in the
   final document, in English or in Arabic. Search the finished draft for each.
3. **Every operative refusal carries "to the maximum extent permitted by applicable Saudi law".** Not
   as decoration: it is the phrase that lets a tribunal read a clause down rather than strike it.
4. **Every deeming clause carries a plain English lead in bold.** The live policy already does this and
   it materially reduces the "unfair surprise" attack surface.
5. **Every list carries "including, by way of illustration and not of limitation".** Section 13 of the
   live policy already makes "including" non-exhaustive; repeat it locally in the long lists anyway.
6. **No em dashes, no en dashes.** Check the finished English and the finished Arabic with a search for
   the two characters before publishing. Hyphens inside compounds (non-refundable, Auto-Apply,
   cross-border) are fine.
7. **Never write that a job, an interview, or an offer is promised, likely, or expected.** Use the live
   form: "does not guarantee interviews, offers, or employment".
8. **Merchant identity string, exactly:** Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات). Use it at the
   load bearing points (the deeming clause, the no refund clause, the chargeback clause, the correction
   clause, governing law), not in every sentence, matching the live density.
9. **Arabic parity is clause-for-clause, not paragraph-for-paragraph.** The Arabic version prevails
   under live Section 13. Any concept that exists only in the English text is a concept that does not
   exist. Deemed delivery, deemed acceptance, availability as the measure of performance, the standing
   advance consent, the anti-characterisation sweep, the ex gratia clause, and the anti-assignment
   clause each need a specific Arabic operative term chosen and then used consistently.
10. **Do not draft an interest or penalty clause.** Documented costs actually incurred only.
11. **Do not draft a contra proferentem exclusion.** It will not hold in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia
    and it makes the document read as adversarial.
12. **Do not draft anything that appears to bar a complaint to a competent authority.** Keep the
    express preservation in Section 12.1.

# Appendix B. Residual risks the drafters should know about

Ranked by how likely each is to cause an actual loss.

1. **The digital content withdrawal exception is evidenced by access, not by availability.** The Saudi
   e-commerce framework excludes used or opened digital content from the withdrawal right. A merchant
   asserting "availability is performance" against a consumer who can show they never logged in is
   relying on the contractual test rather than the statutory one. Mitigation is operational: provision
   entitlements at the instant of charge, log it, and keep the cascade at Section 3.7 in the document.
2. **Total unavailability for an entire paid period.** This is the one fact pattern where a Saudi
   tribunal applying general principles is likely to intervene whatever the drafting says, because the
   customer received nothing. Mitigation is the substitute-access discretion at optional Section 10.4
   and an incident process, not more words.
3. **A stale promise elsewhere in the estate.** Deleting the score improvement guarantee from the
   Policy while it survives in the Terms (Section 7.7), on the CV Enhancer landing page, in ads, or in
   marketing emails converts a removed clause into a misleading advertising exposure. Sweep every
   surface in the same release.
4. **The cancellation claim in Section 4.3 must be factually true.** If cancellation is not genuinely
   self-serve and available at any time, that clause is a false statement of fact in a published
   policy. Verify in the product before publishing.
5. **A seven day time bar running from the charge date alone.** Very likely to be read down for a
   consumer who sees the charge on a monthly statement. Use the layered version in Section 14.1.
6. **"Conclusive evidence" without a manifest error carve-out.** The most commonly struck evidential
   clause. Keep the carve-out.
7. **Existing subscribers.** They accepted the old policy. Without the transitional clause at 11.O plus
   an email notice before the effective date, the advance consent in Section 3.6 has not been obtained
   from the existing base, which is exactly the population that generates renewal disputes.
8. **Checkout evidence.** Card scheme rules expect the refund policy to be displayed at the point of
   sale and the merchant to be able to prove how and where. The strongest chargeback clause in the
   world loses without that screenshot in the Records.
9. **Ten business days for a correction.** Can exceed the fourteen day expectation. Cap it.
10. **Class-wide regulatory action rather than individual claims.** The individual-claim clause at
    15.11 does nothing against a regulator acting on aggregated complaints. The defence against that is
    not textual: it is a low complaint rate, a five day response commitment that is actually met, and a
    policy that reads as firm rather than as punitive.
