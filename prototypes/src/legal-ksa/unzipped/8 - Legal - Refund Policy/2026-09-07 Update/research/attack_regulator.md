# REGULATORY REVIEW: Uptal Refund Policy (draft for publication at https://uptal.com/refund-policy)

**Reviewing posture:** Ministry of Commerce, E-Commerce Supervision (Article 16, E-Commerce Law), with a
parallel CST/CITC and SDAIA read on the data and channel provisions.

**Instruments applied:**
- E-Commerce Law, Royal Decree M/126 of 7/11/1440H ("the Law")
- Implementing Regulations of the E-Commerce Law, as amended 22 March 2021 ("the Regulations")
- Civil Transactions Law, Royal Decree M/191 of 29/11/1444H ("CTL")
- Law of Evidence, Royal Decree M/43 of 26/5/1443H ("Evidence Law")
- SAMA Implementing Regulations of the Payments and Payment Services Law, Part 6 ("SAMA IR")
- VAT Implementing Regulations (ZATCA), price display rules
- MoC E-Store Guide (الدليل الإرشادي للمتاجر الإلكترونية) and standing MoC enforcement practice on
  "البضاعة المباعة لا ترد ولا تستبدل"
- Personal Data Protection Law ("PDPL")

**Powers engaged if published as drafted:** Article 18 of the Law (warning; fine up to SAR 1,000,000;
temporary or permanent suspension of e-commerce activity; partial or total blocking of the electronic
store) and Article 17 (urgent blocking pending committee decision within ten days).

---

## 0. OVERALL VERDICT

The document is not refused for reaching a no-refund commercial outcome. A Saudi merchant may lawfully
sell a digital service on final-payment terms, and the drafter has correctly identified the load-bearing
ground: Article 13(1) of the Law conditions the seven day rescission right on the consumer not having used
the service or obtained a benefit from it. That is a lawful architecture.

It is refused for three things it does on top of that outcome.

**First, it converts a factual defence into a contractual deeming and then makes the deeming
counterfactual.** Section 3.1 states in terms that the rules apply "irrespective of whether any of the
matters described in this Section ... in fact occurs in your case". Article 13(1) turns on a fact. A term
that says the fact is irrelevant is a term that purports to disapply the Article. That is the single
clause that turns a defensible policy into an arbitrary one under Article 96 CTL.

**Second, every mandatory-law savings clause in the document terminates in Section 15.3, which deems the
value of what was supplied to be the whole price in every case.** Section 1.4 (mandatory rights), Section
9.5 (Article 14 delay), Section 14.2 (outage), Section 14.3 (discontinuation), Section 15.12 (capacity and
death) and Section 5.5 (access never opened) are each routed to Section 15.3, and Section 15.3 then
reduces the statutory remedy to zero by a deeming. A savings clause that is engineered to return nothing is
not a savings clause. It is a decoy, and its presence is evidence of design rather than of oversight. A
tribunal that finds this will not read the policy down under Article 96; it will reopen it.

**Third, it removes every channel the consumer might use and then makes the merchant's own record
conclusive about what happened in the one channel that remains.** Cancellation exists only in account
settings; the merchant's Records are conclusive evidence of whether a cancellation occurred; a statement
by the merchant's own staff that a subscription was cancelled is declared "of no effect"; closing the
account does not stop billing; a ticket does not stop billing; and the merchant undertakes no response
time. That combination is an abusive design, independently of the refund rule.

**Disposition:** publication is not permitted in the present form. 7 findings are marked CRITICAL and must
be amended or deleted before publication. 17 are marked HIGH and must be amended within thirty days of
publication. The mandatory disclosure defects in Part 2 must be cured before publication because they are
absolute obligations under Articles 6, 7 and 8 of the Law and Articles 4, 6, 7 and 8 of the Regulations.

Every finding below carries the exact redraft that closes it. In every case the redraft preserves the
no-refund outcome. Not one of them requires the merchant to pay money for a service it supplied.

## PART 1: TERMS I WOULD ORDER DELETED OR AMENDED

---

### R1. Section 3.1, final paragraph. The counterfactual clause. CRITICAL. VOID.

**Text at issue:**
> "The rules established by this Policy apply irrespective of whether any of the matters described in this
> Section, or in any other descriptive provision of this Policy, in fact occurs in your case, and no
> assertion that any such matter did not occur affects the finality of any Charge or gives rise to any
> right, claim, or entitlement."

**Rule:** Article 13(1) of the Law makes the rescission right conditional on a question of fact ("ما دام
أنه لم يستخدم منتج موفر الخدمة أو لم يستفد من خدمته أو لم يحصل على منفعة من أي منهما"). Article 96 CTL
empowers the court to amend or disapply arbitrary terms in an adhesion contract, and voids any agreement to
the contrary. Article 104(3) CTL construes doubt in an adhesion contract in favour of the adhering party.
Article 107 CTL preserves the remedy for non-performance.

**Objection.** Everything else in Section 3 is a representation by the merchant about its own conduct: it
opens access, it issues entitlements, it begins the work. That is exactly the right way to build the case,
because it makes the statutory condition true as a matter of fact. This paragraph then says the conduct
does not have to have happened. It is an express contractual instruction that non-performance is
irrelevant. It also contradicts Section 5.5 of the same document, which does provide a remedy where access
was not in fact opened. Under Article 104(3) that contradiction resolves against the merchant. The
paragraph is void, and worse, it taints Section 3 as a whole by demonstrating that the merchant did not
intend the factual representations to be tested.

**Order:** delete the paragraph. It is not load-bearing. Section 3.9 already provides the cumulative
alternative bases, and Section 5.5 already provides the correct non-monetary answer to non-provisioning.

**Exact redraft (replaces the paragraph):**
> "Sections 3.1 to 3.3 describe what Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) does at the moment of
> each Charge, and Uptal maintains Records of it in accordance with Section 12.1. Where, exceptionally, the
> access, entitlements, or Allowances for a Billing Period were not in fact opened, issued, or made
> operative against a Charge, Section 5.5 applies and provides your sole and exclusive remedy, which is the
> opening of that access or the restarting of the Billing Period. Nothing in this Section requires you to
> demonstrate use as a condition of the Charge being final, and nothing in it makes the finality of a
> Charge depend on the quantity, quality, or existence of any output produced within a Billing Period."

### R2. Section 15.3, final paragraph. The deeming that empties every savings clause. CRITICAL. VOID.

**Text at issue:**
> "For the purposes of paragraph (iii), and to the maximum extent permitted by applicable Saudi law, the
> value of the access, availability, Allowances, and performance in fact provided is deemed to be the whole
> of the price of the Billing Period or order concerned wherever access was opened, an Allowance was issued,
> or a deliverable was produced, irrespective of the extent, quantity, quality, or existence of any output,
> and irrespective of the extent to which the person concerned in fact used what was made available."

**Rule:** Article 96 CTL (arbitrary terms, contrary agreement void). Article 97 CTL (reduction of an
onerous obligation; "يقع باطلًا كل اتفاق على خلاف أحكام هذه المادة"). Article 110(2) CTL (partial and
temporary impossibility in contracts of duration extinguishes the obligation and its counterpart pro
tanto). Article 14 of the Law (delay beyond fifteen days: rescission and recovery of what was paid).
Articles 144, 145 and 148 CTL (restitution of what was not due).

**Objection.** This is the most serious clause in the document. Read it with the provisions that point to
it. Section 1.4(e) routes mandatory rights to Section 15.3. Section 5.5 routes the unprovisionable charge
to Section 15.3. Section 9.5 routes the Article 14 delay remedy to Section 15.3. Section 14.2 routes the
Article 110(2) outage remedy to Section 15.3. Section 14.3 routes discontinuation to Section 15.3. Section
15.12 routes capacity and death to mandatory law and thence to Section 15.3. In every one of those cases,
Section 15.3 then deems the value of what was supplied to be one hundred per cent of the price, so the
statutory abatement or recovery is arithmetically nil. The merchant has written six savings clauses and one
clause that cancels all six.

Article 110(2) is the clearest illustration. A subscription is a contract of duration. A three week outage
on an annual Talent Search plan extinguishes the corresponding part of the fee **by operation of law**.
Section 15.3 says the value provided was the whole price. That is an agreement contrary to Article 110(2)
and, through Article 97, an agreement the Law declares void.

**Order:** delete the paragraph and replace it with a proportionate valuation rule. The merchant loses
nothing in the ordinary case, because in the ordinary case the service was in fact available throughout.

**Exact redraft (replaces the paragraph):**
> "For the purposes of paragraph (iii), the value of the access, availability, Allowances, and performance
> in fact provided is determined by reference to the proportion of the Billing Period during which the
> service was in fact made available to you, and to the Allowances in fact issued and consumed, as evidenced
> by the Records. Where the service was made available throughout the Billing Period, that value is the whole
> of the price of that Billing Period, irrespective of the extent to which you in fact used what was made
> available and irrespective of the results you obtained. This Section does not limit any abatement,
> extinction, or reduction that a provision of Saudi law that is mandatory and cannot be waived, limited, or
> varied by agreement requires, including Article 110(2) of the Civil Transactions Law in respect of a
> contract of duration."

### R3. Section 1.4(b). The savings clause that disapplies itself. CRITICAL. VOID.

**Text at issue:**
> "(b) does not apply to any right that has been validly affected, reduced, or extinguished, including by
> the request and consent you give under Section 3.8 and by the fact that you use the service and obtain a
> benefit from it at the moment of each Charge;"

**Rule:** Article 96 CTL. The defining characteristic of a mandatory provision is that it cannot be waived,
limited, or varied by agreement. A clause that disapplies the savings clause by reference to a consent
given under the same contract is a variation by agreement of the very thing that cannot be varied by
agreement.

**Objection.** The savings clause is the reason a court reads a hard policy down rather than striking it.
Limb (b) destroys it, and does so by pointing at Section 3.8, which is itself a contractual consent. The
circularity is visible on the page: Section 1.4 says "nothing in this Policy takes away a mandatory right",
then says "except where a term of this Policy has taken it away". The consent in Section 3.8 is capable of
defeating Article 13(1) as a matter of fact, because performance actually starts. It is not capable of
"extinguishing" a mandatory right as a matter of agreement, and the policy must not say that it is.

**Order:** delete limb (b) and re-cast the point as a factual statement, not a legal extinction.

**Exact redraft (replaces limb (b)):**
> "(b) is concerned with rights that subsist. It does not preserve a right whose statutory precondition is
> not satisfied as a matter of fact, and in particular does not preserve a right of rescission under Article
> 13(1) of the Saudi E-Commerce Law where you have used the service, benefited from it, or obtained a
> benefit from it, that being a question of fact and not a matter of agreement;"

### R4. Section 3.8, third paragraph. Purported extinction of the Article 13 right by agreement. CRITICAL.

**Text at issue:**
> "any statutory right of rescission, withdrawal, return, or cancellation that you might otherwise have had
> in respect of that Charge, including under Article 13(1) of the Saudi E-Commerce Law, is thereby wholly
> and irrevocably extinguished, to the maximum extent permitted by applicable Saudi law"

**Rule:** Article 13(1) of the Law; Article 96 CTL; Article 7(1)(a) of the Regulations.

**Objection.** The same defect as R3, in the operative clause rather than the savings clause. A consumer
cannot contract out of Article 13, and a clause that says a statutory right is "wholly and irrevocably
extinguished" by the consumer's own consent will be struck as arbitrary even though the merchant's
underlying factual position is sound. The identical commercial result is achieved by saying that the
statutory condition is not met. Say that instead. Note also Section 1.5, second paragraph, which frames the
same point correctly ("no right to rescind arises"): the two paragraphs are inconsistent, and Article 104(3)
resolves the inconsistency against the merchant.

**Exact redraft (replaces the quoted words):**
> "the condition on which any right of rescission under Article 13(1) of the Saudi E-Commerce Law depends,
> namely that you have not used the service, have not benefited from it, and have not obtained any benefit
> from it, is not satisfied in respect of that Charge, and no such right accordingly arises. Uptal
> (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) does not purport to exclude, waive, or extinguish any right
> conferred by that Article, and this Section records the factual position disclosed to you before payment
> in accordance with Article 7(1)(a) of the Implementing Regulations of that Law."

### R5. Section 6.8. Denial of the Article 4 error-correction right. CRITICAL. VOID. AND A DISCLOSURE GAP.

**Text at issue:**
> "the purchase of a plan, product, term, billing frequency, seat count, or currency other than the one you
> intended, the purchase of a longer term than you wanted, the purchase of one product in the belief that it
> was another, and any purchase made in error, in haste, or on the wrong account;"

**Rule:** **Article 4 of the Law**: where the consumer makes an error in an electronic communication and the
communication technology did not enable him to correct it, he may notify the service provider of the error
immediately upon becoming aware of it, within the period fixed by the Regulations, and that notification
constitutes correction of the error **provided the consumer has not used the product or service or obtained
a benefit from either**. **Article 4 of the Regulations**: the consumer's correction is accepted if he
notifies the provider **within twenty four (24) hours** of sending the communication, and "**يتعين على موفر
الخدمة تصحيح الخطأ المبلغ عنه**" (the service provider **is required to correct** the notified error) unless
the consumer has benefited. Article 4(3) of the Regulations gives the consumer, where the **provider's** own
electronic communication contained an unintentional error, the choice between proceeding after correction and
rescinding **with recovery of what he paid**.

**Objection.** This is a mandatory statutory correction right, it is expressly directed at exactly the
mis-purchase scenario the bullet describes, and the policy denies it in terms. Worse, the words "Article 4"
do not appear anywhere in the 30,000 word document. The drafter has covered Articles 7, 10, 11, 13 and 14
and has missed the one article that speaks directly to the most common consumer complaint a subscription
merchant receives. The bullet is void as against a consumer who notifies within twenty four hours and has
not obtained a benefit, and its presence is an independent breach of Article 7(1)(a) of the Regulations,
because the pre-contract statement is required to state the consumer's position accurately.

Note also the interaction with R1: the merchant will say the consumer obtained a benefit at the instant of
charge. That is a fact to be proved, not a term to be asserted, and where the consumer mis-selects a plan
and reports it in minutes the merchant will not prove it.

**Order:** amend the bullet and add a new operative subsection at Section 4 giving effect to Article 4.

**Exact redraft (replaces the bullet):**
> "the purchase of a plan, product, term, billing frequency, seat count, or currency other than the one you
> intended, save where Section 4.15 applies;"

**New Section 4.15, to be inserted:**
> "### 4.15 Correction of an error made at checkout
>
> **In plain terms: if you selected the wrong plan, term, seat count or currency at checkout and you tell
> Uptal within 24 hours, and you have not used the service, Uptal corrects the order to the one you
> intended. That is a correction of an order, not a refund.**
>
> Where you made an error in the electronic communication by which you placed an order, the checkout did not
> enable you to correct it before submission, and you notify Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات)
> of the error by a Ticket at https://uptal.com/contact-us within twenty four (24) hours of that
> communication, Uptal will correct the notified error in accordance with Article 4 of the Saudi E-Commerce
> Law and Article 4 of its Implementing Regulations, unless you have by then used the service, benefited
> from it, or obtained a benefit from it. Correction is effected by moving your order or Subscription to the
> plan, term, billing frequency, seat count, or currency you intended, and by adjusting the amount charged
> to the amount properly payable for it, any excess being returned to the original payment method and to the
> Payer of Record only. That adjustment is a correction of an order that was never the order you placed. It
> is not a refund of any Uptal service, is not a Correction under Section 10, and is not an exception to
> Section 5.1.
>
> Where Uptal's own electronic communication to you contained an unintentional error, Uptal will notify you
> of it as soon as it becomes aware of it and before performance begins, and you may then elect to continue
> after correction or to rescind and recover what you paid, in accordance with Article 4(3) of those
> Regulations."

### R6. Section 12.2. Conclusive evidence, in particular of cancellation. CRITICAL.

**Text at issue:**
> "they constitute conclusive evidence of the fact, amount, currency, time, and authorisation of each
> Charge, of the moment of Initiation of each Charge, of the opening of access, of the entitlements and
> Allowances issued to your account, of the work performed for you, of the commercial terms disclosed to you
> at the point of sale, of the disclosures and consents presented to and accepted by you, of the version of
> this Policy in force at the time of each Charge, and of the date and content of every cancellation,
> cancellation attempt, pause, Ticket, and other communication"

**Rule:** Article 6 of the Evidence Law permits the parties to agree rules of evidence in writing **unless
the agreement offends public order**. Article 57 of the Evidence Law gives non-official digital evidence the
status of proof against the parties **"ما لم يثبت العكس"** (unless the contrary is proved). Article 58
places the burden on the party alleging invalidity. Article 96 CTL.

**Objection.** The Evidence Law already gives Uptal a presumption and already places the burden on the
customer. Section 12.2 asks for more than the statute gives, and in doing so trades a valid clause for one
that will be amended. That is a drafting own goal on most of the listed matters.

On one listed matter it is not merely an own goal, it is abusive. This policy makes the self-service control
in the merchant's own product the **only** way to cancel (Sections 4.3, 4.5, 4.6, 12.6, 17). It then makes
the merchant's own record **conclusive** of "the date and content of every cancellation, cancellation
attempt". The merchant therefore controls the sole cancellation channel, the sole log of it, and the
evidential status of that log. A consumer who cancelled and was charged anyway has, by contract, no means of
proving it. That is a term I would strike in any adhesion contract, and it is the term most likely to
produce a Ministry complaint file.

The same objection attaches to Section 1.9 ("Uptal's records of the version of this Policy in force at the
time of any Charge are conclusive evidence of that matter") and Section 12.6 ("Uptal's ticket records are
conclusive evidence of that date").

**Order:** replace "conclusive evidence" with the Evidence Law formulation throughout, in Sections 12.2, 1.9
and 12.6. Retain the second and third paragraphs of Section 12.2, which are correctly drafted.

**Exact redraft (replaces the quoted words in Section 12.2):**
> "they are evidence, as between you and Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات), of the fact, amount,
> currency, time, and authorisation of each Charge, of the moment of Initiation of each Charge, of the
> opening of access, of the entitlements and Allowances issued to your account, of the work performed for
> you, of the commercial terms disclosed to you at the point of sale, of the disclosures and consents
> presented to and accepted by you, of the version of this Policy in force at the time of each Charge, and
> of the date and content of every cancellation, cancellation attempt, pause, Ticket, and other
> communication, unless the contrary is proved, in accordance with Articles 57 and 58 of the Saudi Law of
> Evidence. Where you assert that you completed a cancellation and Uptal's Records do not show it, Uptal
> will search its cancellation, session, and error logs for the period you identify and will provide you
> with the result of that search."

Apply the same substitution ("is evidence of that matter unless the contrary is proved") in Sections 1.9 and
12.6.

### R7. Section 13.1. Prices displayed to consumers exclusive of VAT. CRITICAL.

**Text at issue:**
> "Prices are stated either exclusive or inclusive of Value Added Tax (VAT), as indicated at the point of
> sale for the relevant product. Where a price is stated exclusive of VAT, applicable VAT (currently 15%) is
> added at checkout"

**Rule:** **Article 7(d) of the Law**: the pre-contract statement must include "إجمالي السعر شاملاً جميع
الرسوم أو الضرائب أو المبالغ الإضافية" (the total price **including all fees, taxes** and any additional
amounts). **Article 7(1)(b) of the Regulations**: the price detail, the original price, delivery costs, and
taxes, plus any other future costs and how they are calculated. The VAT Implementing Regulations require
prices displayed to the public to be VAT inclusive.

**Objection.** Auto-Apply and CV Enhancer are sold to natural persons. Displaying a job seeker a price
exclusive of VAT and adding fifteen per cent at checkout is a display breach under Article 7(d) and under
the VAT price display rule, independently of anything in this policy. The clause as drafted licenses that
practice for every product. It is also the single most common e-store finding the Ministry makes.

**Order:** amend so that every price displayed to a natural person is VAT inclusive; the exclusive option is
available only for business subscribers on Talent Search, and even then the total inclusive price must be
shown before payment.

**Exact redraft (replaces the first two sentences of Section 13.1):**
> "Every price displayed to you before payment is the total price, inclusive of Value Added Tax (VAT,
> currently 15%) and of every other fee, tax, and additional amount payable, in accordance with Article 7 of
> the Saudi E-Commerce Law. Where a plan is offered to a business subscriber and a price is additionally
> shown exclusive of VAT for accounting convenience, the VAT inclusive total is displayed alongside it and
> is the amount charged. VAT is applied in accordance with the tax regulations of the Kingdom of Saudi
> Arabia as in force from time to time."

### R8. Section 12.3. Reversal of the burden of proof and an imposed standard of evidence. HIGH.

**Text at issue:**
> "Any person asserting that a Charge was not made, that access or availability was not provided, that an
> Allowance was not issued, that a cancellation was completed, or that a service was not performed, bears the
> burden of establishing that assertion by contemporaneous documentary evidence."

and

> "The absence of a contemporaneous report is evidence that the product was available and functioning during
> the period in question"

**Rule:** Article 6 of the Evidence Law (agreements on evidence must not offend public order). Article 96
CTL. Article 14(2) of the Law, which places the duty to notify of any expected delay or material difficulty
in performance **on the service provider**, not on the consumer.

**Objection.** Two separate defects. First, the clause does not merely allocate the burden, it dictates the
**means** of discharging it, excluding testimony, presumption, and the merchant's own disclosure. A consumer
who cancelled at 23:58 on a phone and was charged at 00:01 holds no document. Under this clause he loses
automatically. That is an agreement on evidence that offends public order and will not be given effect.
Second, converting the consumer's silence into affirmative evidence for the merchant inverts Article 14(2),
under which it is the merchant who must speak about interruptions. The merchant is the only party with
uptime data.

**Exact redraft (replaces both quoted passages):**
> "Where you assert that a Charge was not made, that access or availability was not provided, that an
> Allowance was not issued, that a cancellation was completed, or that a service was not performed, you
> should provide such evidence as is available to you, and Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات)
> will provide the corresponding entries from the Records. Each party may prove its case by any means
> admissible under the Saudi Law of Evidence.
>
> Where you become aware that a product is unavailable, inaccessible, or not functioning, you should report
> it by Ticket at https://uptal.com/contact-us while it is continuing, so that it can be investigated and,
> where possible, resolved. A contemporaneous report assists Uptal in establishing what occurred; the absence
> of one does not by itself determine the question, which is decided on the Records and on any other evidence
> available. Uptal will notify you of any expected delay or difficulty having a material effect on the
> delivery or performance of a service, in accordance with Article 14(2) of the Saudi E-Commerce Law."

### R9. Sections 12.7, 12.8 and 6.9. A contractual claim bar dressed as a process condition. HIGH. VOID IN PART.

**Text at issue (Section 12.8):**
> "Each Charge, and each tax invoice issued in respect of it, is treated as correct, accepted, and free from
> objection upon the expiry of the applicable period in Section 12.7 without a compliant Ticket having been
> submitted"

**Text at issue (Section 6.9, bullet):**
> "the fact that a claim was raised outside the periods stated in Section 10.4 or Section 12.7;"

**Rule:** Article 305(1) CTL: "لا يجوز الاتفاق على تقصير مدة عدم سماع الدعوى، ولا على إطالتها." It is not
permitted to agree to shorten or to lengthen the period for the non-hearing of a claim. Articles 144, 145,
148 and 159 CTL (restitution of what was not due; heard for three years from knowledge and in any case ten
years from the right arising).

**Objection.** The drafter knows about Article 305 and has written a saving in the second paragraph of
Section 12.8 and in Section 10.4. Those savings are good. But the first paragraph of Section 12.8 then
deems the Charge "correct, accepted, and free from objection" on expiry, and Section 6.9 lists lateness as a
substantive non-ground. A deemed acceptance on expiry of a contractual window is a limitation period by
another name, and it operates on the restitution claim for a duplicate charge, which has a three year and a
ten year statutory life. The saving and the deeming cannot both stand; under Article 104(3) the
inconsistency resolves against the merchant, and the safer course for the merchant is to remove the deeming
and keep the process condition, which is enforceable.

**Order:** delete the deemed acceptance sentence and the Section 6.9 bullet.

**Exact redraft (replaces the quoted sentence in Section 12.8):**
> "Compliance with Section 12.7 is a condition of Uptal's internal review process and a condition precedent
> to the consideration by Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) of any claim in respect of a Charge
> under that process. A Ticket submitted after the applicable period concludes Uptal's obligation to
> investigate under this Policy. It does not affect, and this Policy does not purport to affect, any period
> prescribed by law for bringing a claim before a competent court, for making a complaint to a competent
> authority, or for making any request to a bank, card issuer, payment provider, or card scheme."

**Exact redraft (replaces the Section 6.9 bullet):**
> "the fact that a claim was raised outside the periods stated in Section 10.4 or Section 12.7, which
> concludes Uptal's obligation to investigate it under this Policy and does not affect any period prescribed
> by law;"

### R10. Sections 11.1 and 12.9 read together. A procedural trap. HIGH.

**Text at issue (Section 11.1):**
> "you must allow Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) a reasonable opportunity to review and
> respond, being not less than ten (10) Business Days from the date the Ticket is received."

**Text at issue (Section 12.9):**
> "Those periods are not suspended, extended, tolled, postponed, restarted, or renewed by: any
> correspondence, discussion, or negotiation with Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) or its
> support team; any investigation being conducted; any request for further information; the filing of a
> chargeback, payment dispute, or complaint with any bank, card issuer, payment provider, card scheme, or
> third party; the making of a complaint to any authority"

**Rule:** Article 95 CTL (performance in good faith). Article 96 CTL. Article 305(1) CTL.

**Objection.** Ten Business Days in the Kingdom is two calendar weeks. The Section 10.4 window for a
duplicate charge is thirty calendar days. A consumer who discovers a duplicate charge on day twenty must
first open a ticket, must then wait fourteen days before he is permitted to approach his bank, and Section
12.9 tells him that the clock does not stop while he waits. He complies with the policy and loses his rights
under the policy. That is a trap, and a term operating as a trap is arbitrary under Article 96 and a breach
of the good faith obligation in Article 95. It also cannot stand against Article 305 to the extent that it
purports to prevent tolling of any statutory period.

**Order:** suspend the internal windows for the duration of Uptal's own review.

**Exact redraft (add as a new final paragraph of Section 12.9, and amend the opening words of the quoted
Section 12.9 passage to read "Save as provided in the final paragraph of this Section, those periods are not
suspended ..."):**
> "The periods in Section 10.4 and Section 12.7 are suspended from the date a compliant Ticket relating to
> the Charge is received by the Uptal ticketing system until the date Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية
> المعلومات) communicates its substantive determination to you or the period in Section 11.1 expires,

### R11. Sections 11.2 and 11.3. Filing a payment dispute treated as a material breach. HIGH.

**Text at issue:**
> "The filing of a chargeback, payment dispute, reversal request, or claim of non-receipt or unauthorised
> use with any bank, card issuer, payment provider, card scheme, installment or wallet provider, mobile

without notice" and "whether pending or following its investigation", that is, before any determination that

### R14. Section 4.4 with the definition of "Initiated" in Section 2.1. A merchant-controlled cut-off with no outer limit. HIGH.

**Text at issue (Section 2.1):**
> "**"Initiated"** means, in relation to a Charge, the moment at which Uptal's billing systems create the
> transaction record for that Charge and submit it for authorisation, as recorded in the Records"

**Text at issue (Section 4.4):**
> "To prevent the next Renewal Charge, the cancellation must be completed and recorded in Uptal's systems
> before that Charge is Initiated, and you are advised to complete it not less than twenty four (24) hours
> before your Renewal Date and time as shown in your account."

**Rule:** Article 96 CTL. Article 104(3) CTL. Article 7(1)(d) and (e) of the Regulations (contract term,
expiry, and recurring payments must be disclosed).

**Objection.** The operative cut-off is Initiation, which is defined entirely by reference to the merchant's
own systems and is not disclosed to the consumer, is not shown in the account, and is subject to no stated

told his Renewal Date, is "advised" to cancel twenty four hours before it, and is then bound by a different,

merchant, and Article 96 catches a deadline that only one party can see or move. Read with Section 12.2

listed among the things that do not cancel a Subscription, "pause billing, stop or delay a renewal, or have
any effect whatsoever on any Charge".

**Rule:** **Article 5(3) of the Regulations**: where the consumer's relationship with the e-store is

### R20. Section 9.5. No delivery date is given at all. HIGH.

**Text at issue:**
> "No delivery date, delivery deadline, or turnaround period is given or guaranteed for an Uptal CV Enhancer
> order, and any indicative timeframe communicated to you is an estimate only."

**Rule:** **Article 7(e) of the Law**: the pre-contract statement must state the arrangements for payment,

recovery of the price. The clause therefore breaches a disclosure obligation and simultaneously imports the
statutory remedy the merchant is trying to avoid. Stating a period, and meeting it, is strictly better for
the merchant.

### R25. Section 15.12. Capacity, minors, and money taken under a contract that never existed. HIGH.

**Text at issue:**

That is untenable and it is the kind of finding that attracts an Article 18 penalty rather than an amendment
direction. State the correct position plainly; it costs the merchant almost nothing, because the population

### R27. Section 13.2. "Re-entry or replacement of your payment details" as a refreshed authorisation. MEDIUM HIGH.

**Text at issue:**

**Rule:** **SAMA IR Article 90(1)**: the payer is entitled to a refund from the payee's payment service

**Objection.** The rest of Section 13.2 is well conceived and I record that: fixing the authorised amount at

### R40. Article 6(b) of the Law: reducing "means of contact" to a single web form. HIGH.

**Text at issue (Section 12.6):**
> "Email to any address, social media messages, messages sent through WhatsApp or any other messaging
> application, telephone calls, in-product chat, and messages to individual employees, officers, agents,
> contractors, or representatives do not constitute notice for the purposes of this Policy"

**Rule:** Article 6(b) of the Law requires disclosure of the provider's **means of contact** (plural,
"وسائل الاتصال به"). Article 15(1)(a) of the Regulations, on authentication data, likewise requires "وسائل
التواصل معه".

**Objection.** Nominating one channel as the exclusive channel for formal notice is acceptable, and I do not
object to it. Publishing a policy under which no other channel exists at all, in a document that
simultaneously retires the merchant's published email address, is not. A consumer whose account is locked and
who cannot log in to the form, or who has no internet access to the form, has no route to the merchant. The
redraft at R39 cures this: keep the ticket as the exclusive channel for **notice**, and publish at least one
other means of **contact**.

### R41. Article 7(2) of the Regulations: the pay button. HIGH.

**Rule:** "على موفر الخدمة أن يوضح للمستهلك الإجراء الذي يترتب عليه إبرام العقد، بأن يذكر في الخانة المخصصة
ما يفيد أنه بمجرد النقر عليها يكون العقد قد أبرم، ويترتب على ذلك الالتزام بالدفع، وذلك بطريقة واضحة ويسيرة."
The pay control itself must state that clicking it concludes the contract and creates the payment obligation.

**Objection.** Section 1.5 enumerates what is shown before payment: price, currency, tax treatment, Billing
Period, whether it renews, frequency, means of cancellation, and this Policy. The Article 7(2) statement is
not among them. Since the merchant is relying on Section 1.5 as its record of pre-contract compliance, the
omission is material.

**Exact redraft (add to the first sentence of Section 1.5, after "and this Policy"):**
> ", and the payment control itself states that by clicking it the contract is concluded and you become
> obliged to pay, in accordance with Article 7(2) of the Implementing Regulations of the Saudi E-Commerce
> Law"

### R42. Article 8 of the Law and Article 8 of the Regulations: invoice contents. MEDIUM.

**Rule:** The invoice must be capable of being retained and must include, among other things: confirmation of
the conclusion of the contract and its date; the total price and the tax amount with details of how the price
is calculated and the conditions of its accrual; **the provider's tax number**; **the date of delivery of the
product or of the provision of the service**; and **a summary of the exchange and return provisions where
applicable**.

**Objection.** Section 13.8 provides only that "a VAT-compliant tax invoice is made available in your Uptal
account". VAT compliance under the ZATCA rules is not the same as compliance with Article 8 of the
Regulations. The two additional items that are certainly missing are the service date and the summary of the
return provisions.

**Exact redraft (replaces the first sentence of Section 13.8):**
> "A tax invoice is made available in your Uptal account in respect of each Charge, in a form capable of
> being retained, in accordance with the tax regulations of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia and with Article 8 of
> the Saudi E-Commerce Law and Article 8 of its Implementing Regulations. Each invoice states the product or
> service purchased, confirmation of the conclusion of the contract and its date, the total price and the
> amount of Value Added Tax, Uptal's tax registration number, the date on which the service was provided or
> the deliverable delivered, the payment method and confirmation of payment, and a summary of this Policy's
> provisions on returns and exchanges."

### R43. Article 7(1)(b) of the Regulations: how future costs are calculated. MEDIUM.

**Rule:** the pre-contract statement must set out any other costs that may be calculated in future, **with a
statement of how they are calculated and the conditions of their accrual**.

**Objection.** Two categories of future cost appear in this policy and neither is quantified or explained:
the dispute recovery in Section 11.3(e) (addressed at R12), and a future price change under Section 13.2. On
the second, "prices may change at any time and from time to time, without prior notice" is legitimate for new
purchases and I do not disturb it, but it must be paired with the statement, which Section 13.2 already
substantially makes, that a change does not affect an existing mandate until a refreshed authorisation.
Bring that pairing into Section 1.5 so it forms part of the pre-contract statement.

**Exact redraft (add to Section 1.5, as a new final sentence of the first paragraph):**
> "You are also shown, before you pay, that Uptal may change its prices at any time for new purchases, and
> that a change to the price of a plan does not change the amount charged on the renewals of a Subscription
> you already hold until the new price has been displayed to you and affirmatively accepted by you, as
> provided in Section 13.2."

## PART 3: THE AGGREGATE FINDING

Article 96 CTL is applied to the contract, not only to the clause. A tribunal asked whether this policy
contains arbitrary terms will notice the following pattern and will treat it as design:

1. The consumer may cancel only through one control, in the merchant's product (4.3).
2. Nothing else is a cancellation, including closing the account, which the Regulations guarantee him (4.5).
3. Nobody at the merchant may cancel for him, and a written statement from the merchant that it was cancelled
   is "of no effect" (4.6, 15.6).
4. No reminder is sent and none is owed (4.3, 4.13, 8.2).
5. The operative cut-off is Initiation, defined by the merchant's systems, with no outer limit (2.1, 4.4).
6. The merchant's own record of all of the above is conclusive (12.2).
7. The consumer must prove any contrary account by contemporaneous documentary evidence (12.3).
8. He must complain within thirty days, may not go to his bank for the first fourteen, and the clock does not
   stop while he waits (10.4, 11.1, 12.7, 12.9).
9. On expiry the Charge is deemed correct and free from objection (12.8).
10. If he goes to his bank first, that is a material breach carrying termination and forfeiture (11.2, 11.3).
11. Every mandatory right he might have is preserved in terms (1.4) and then valued at nil (15.3).

Any one of these is arguable. Together they describe a closed system with no exit. That is the finding that
would decide an Article 96 case, and it is also the finding that would decide whether the Ministry issues an
amendment direction or proceeds under Article 18.

The good news for the merchant is that the eleven items are not equally load-bearing. Implementing R2, R6,
R10, R14, R15 and R16 breaks the pattern completely, and none of those six requires the merchant to refund
a single Riyal for a service it supplied. R2 restores proportion to the savings clauses. R6 restores the
statutory evidential standard the merchant already enjoys. R10 removes the timing trap. R14 puts a disclosed
outer limit on a deadline only the merchant can see. R15 links account closure to cancellation. R16 makes the
merchant's own written word count for stopping future billing and nothing else. After those six, the policy
reads as a hard commercial position lawfully expressed, which is what the merchant is entitled to.

**Additional directions:**

1. **Arabic parity.** Section 19 makes the Arabic version prevail. Every amendment ordered above must be made
   in the Arabic version in identical scope. A limb present in English and absent in Arabic is, by the
   merchant's own Section 19, an inconsistency to be resolved in favour of the Arabic, which means an
   omission in Arabic silently deletes the English protection. The Arabic rendering of R35 in particular will
   be assessed against the standing enforcement practice on "البضاعة المباعة لا ترد ولا تستبدل".
2. **Maroof.** The return and refund policy on the Maroof register must be updated in the same release. A
   divergence between the Maroof entry, the Arabic page and the English page is a self-proving finding.
3. **Advertising.** Article 10 of the Law makes every electronic advertisement a contractual document binding
   on the parties, and Article 12 permits the Ministry to require removal of a non-compliant advertisement
   within one day of notice. Every campaign asset, landing page, help centre article and email must be
   scrubbed before publication for any score improvement guarantee, money back guarantee, satisfaction
   guarantee, risk free wording, any promise of a job, an interview or an offer, and any "cancel anytime,
   full refund" phrasing. A surviving guarantee in an advertisement defeats Section 9.4 of the policy by
   operation of Article 10.
4. **Checkout.** The Article 7(1)(a) statement (no rescission right, and why) and the Article 7(2) pay button
   statement must be present at checkout, as an unticked mandatory acknowledgement presented before payment,
   with the timestamp, the policy version, and the language shown recorded. The exact recurring amount and
   interval must be displayed and the acceptance retained, which is also what defeats limb (a) of SAMA IR
   Article 90 on every renewal.
5. **The cancellation control.** Because self-service is the sole route, the control must exist, be reachable
   without contacting anyone, and function; every cancellation event, including failed and abandoned attempts,
   must be timestamped and logged; and account recovery and password reset must work reliably, since a locked
   out subscriber otherwise has no way to stop billing. Section 4.9 of the policy promises a safe harbour that
   only exists if those logs exist.
6. **The retired email address.** support@uptal.com must not simply be deleted. It must be retained with an
   automatic reply directing the sender to https://uptal.com/contact-us for at least twelve months, and
   messages to it during that period must be treated as received on the date sent for the purposes of R23.
   A consumer who writes to the address the previous contract told him to use, and hears nothing, has a
   complete answer to every timing provision in this policy.
