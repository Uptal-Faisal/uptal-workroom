# ATTACK: the sympathetic customer, and the journalist she talks to

**Attacker:** Nouf. 29. Riyadh. Unemployed eleven months. Subscribed to Auto-Apply because the page
said "You just take the interviews" and "Cancel anytime. You're never locked in." Signed an offer on
a Tuesday. Was charged on the Wednesday. Went to cancel, the charge had already gone. Emailed
support the same hour. Was refused. She is not a lawyer and she is not litigious. She is embarrassed,
and she has 4,000 followers, and a friend at a business desk who is looking for exactly this story.

**What she is testing:** not whether the policy is enforceable. Whether it is *publishable*.
Every finding below is scored on one question: if this sentence is screenshotted next to the Uptal
Auto-Apply hero image, who looks worse.

**Method note.** I read the whole policy, the live English policy it replaces, the live Terms, and the
live product pages at uptal.com/auto-apply, uptal.com/talent-search and uptal.com/contact-us. Several
of the worst findings are not in the policy at all. They are contradictions between the policy and
copy that is live on the site today, which Article 10 of the Saudi E-Commerce Law turns into part of
the contract, a point Section 1.7 of the policy itself concedes.

**Verdict up front.** The legal engineering is excellent and mostly survives me. The document loses
on presentation, not on law. There are roughly eight sentences that are worth nothing legally and
everything to a hostile reader, and there are six genuinely customer-favourable rules buried so deep
that no complaining customer will ever find them. Fixing both costs Uptal no money and no ground.
There are then four places where the policy contradicts live marketing, and those are not
presentation problems, those are losses.

## TIER 1: CRITICAL. These are the article.

---

### 1. The got-a-job clause. Section 6.6, and again at 8.5.

**Quote, Section 6.6:**
> "no longer needing or wanting the service, for any reason, including because you have found or
> accepted a position, have been hired, have obtained employment, have withdrawn from the job market,
> have stopped searching, ... and whether that occurred before, on, or after any Charge, **including on
> the day of, or on any day after, a Renewal Charge**;"

**Quote, Section 8.5:**
> "Being hired, accepting an offer, withdrawing from the job market, or otherwise no longer needing the
> Subscription is likewise not a ground, including where that occurs on the day of, or on any day
> after, a Renewal Charge."

**The attack.** This is the headline and nothing else in the document comes close. A company whose
founder's stated personal mission is to eradicate unemployment has written into its refund policy,
twice, that getting a job is expressly not a reason for a refund, and has gone out of its way to add
that this holds *on the very day you were charged*. The second half of each sentence is what makes it
lethal. Without "including on the day of, or on any day after, a Renewal Charge" it is a boring and
correct term. With it, it reads as a company that pictured the moment a jobless person gets hired,
pictured them going to cancel, and drafted the door shut in advance.

It is also self-inflicted, because the legal content is unavoidable and completely defensible. What
is being punished here is the framing: the clause is written as a refusal of the customer's success
instead of as an instruction about how to avoid the charge.

**Severity: critical.** Load-bearing in substance, gratuitous in form.

**The fix.** Keep the rule, invert the frame from refusal to instruction, delete the flourish, and add
the sentence that actually helps.

Replace the Section 6.6 bullet with:
> "no longer needing or wanting the service, for any reason, including a change in your employment or
> hiring situation, a decision to stop or pause your search or your hiring, a change in your plans, or
> a change of mind, and whether that occurred before, on, or after any Charge;"

Replace the second paragraph of Section 8.5 with:
> "The absence, presence, timing, nature, or quality of any employer response, screening, shortlisting,
> interview, offer, or hire is not a ground for any refund, credit, reversal, extension, compensation,
> or other remedy of any kind. Finding a role is not a ground for the refund of a Charge already made.
> It is a reason to cancel, which you may do yourself at any time in your account settings, with effect
> on future Charges."

And add this plain terms lead to Section 8.5, which is the single highest value sentence you can put
in the document:
> "**In plain terms: if you have found a role, that is the point of this. Cancel in your account
> settings as soon as you no longer need Auto-Apply and you will not be charged again. Your access runs
> to the end of the month you have already paid for. A payment already taken is not refunded, so cancel
> before your Renewal Date, which is shown in your account.**"

Legally identical. Unquotable as cruelty. It reads as instruction, and instruction cannot be screenshotted
as contempt.

### 2. "In the same minute." Sections 4.4, 4.7 and 6.3.

**Quote, Section 4.7:**
> "and cancelling immediately after a Renewal Charge, **including in the same minute, the same hour, or
> the same day as that Charge**."

Repeated at 4.4 ("including one completed in the same minute, the same hour, or the same day as that
Charge") and again at 6.3.

**The attack.** "The same minute" is the tell, and it appears three times. It proves the drafter
imagined the customer at the exact instant of realisation, and closed the door with a flourish. It
buys nothing: Section 4.4 already makes Initiation the cut-off, so every post-Initiation cancellation
is caught by the general rule. This is roughly fifteen words of pure reputational liability, tripled.

I would read this clause aloud. It performs itself.

**Severity: critical.** Zero legal value, maximum quotability.

**The fix.** Delete the phrase from all three places and let "after that Charge has been Initiated" do
the work it already does. Then add, in Section 4.4, the sentence that answers the reader's actual
question:
> "If you cancel after a Renewal Charge has been taken, that cancellation stops the next Charge, and
> your access continues to the end of the Billing Period that Charge paid for."

### 3. You told employed users their search was private. Section 8.6 against the live Auto-Apply page.

**Quote, live at uptal.com/auto-apply, FAQ "I'm currently employed. Can my search stay private?":**
> "Absolutely. Your search runs quietly in the background, and applications go only to employers you've
> approved, so you can explore new roles without broadcasting that you're looking."

**Quote, Policy Section 8.6:**
> "may accordingly submit applications to positions, employers, or entities that you have not
> individually reviewed, would not have selected manually, or do not want, **including your current
> employer**, a former employer, a competitor, a client, or any entity you wished to exclude ... **No
> exclusion, blocklist, filter, or targeting preference is warranted to be applied, complete, or
> effective.**"

And Section 8.6 names the consequence it is disclaiming:
> "including any effect on your current employment, your relationships, your reputation, or your
> prospects, entitles you to any refund"

**The attack.** This is the one that ends careers, and it is the one I would lead with if the got-a-job
clause were not available. The site tells a quietly-looking employee that applications go only to
employers they approved. The policy says applications may go to their current employer, that no
exclusion is guaranteed to work, and that if that costs them their job it is not a ground for
anything. Article 10 of the E-Commerce Law makes the advertisement a contractual document
supplementing the contract, which Section 1.7 of the policy concedes in terms. So on this point the
advertisement is the senior document and the policy clause is the weaker of the two.

I do not need a lawyer for this story. I need one person who got outed to their boss.

**Severity: critical.** Not a presentation problem. A live contradiction with a binding advertisement.

**The fix, two moves. The marketing move is the load-bearing one.**

Rewrite the live FAQ answer to:
> "Your search runs quietly in the background. In auto-apply mode the AI applies to matching roles
> without checking with you on each one, so before you start, add any employer you want excluded,
> including your current one, in your account. Exclusions apply to applications sent after you set
> them. If you would rather approve every application yourself, switch auto-apply off and review each
> match one at a time."

Then amend Section 8.6 so the policy matches, and add the one commitment that defuses this entirely
(engineering must confirm it is true before it ships):
> "Exclusions recorded in your account are applied to Submissions made after they are recorded, and
> Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) does not knowingly submit an application to an employer
> recorded as excluded in your account at the time of that Submission. No exclusion, blocklist, filter,
> or targeting preference is warranted to be complete or effective in every case, and you should not
> rely on any of them where disclosure of your search would cause you harm."

### 4. The word "guarantee", live on the product page, against Section 3.3.

**Quotes, live at uptal.com/auto-apply:**
> "**You just take the interviews.**"
> "3 Interviews land in your inbox. **You stay hands-free while the offers come to you.**"
> "Does Uptal guarantee a job? No. Uptal does not guarantee a job, a job offer, or an interview.
> **What Uptal does guarantee is that it keeps searching for suitable roles and applying to them with a
> CV tailored to each one.**"

**Quote, Policy Section 3.3 and 8.1:**
> "Delivery is constituted by the availability of the Subscription to your account for the Billing
> Period, **and not by the number of matches, tailored CVs, applications, or submissions produced**"

**The attack.** The page uses the word *guarantee* to promise the exact output the policy says is not
part of performance. Under Article 10 that guarantee supplements the contract. So a subscriber charged
for a month in which the system submitted zero applications holds an advertised guarantee of applying
against a policy saying output is irrelevant. That is the precise scenario Annex B.2 identifies as the
one case where the central legal theory is paper only, and the advertising has helpfully armed it.

Separately, "You just take the interviews" and "the offers come to you" are promises of interviews and
offers in headline copy. That breaches the founder's own standing rule never to promise a job, an
interview or an offer, it contradicts Section 8.5, and it hands an Article 10 claim to every subscriber
who received neither.

**Severity: critical.** Article 10 exposure plus a breach of the house no-guarantee rule, live today.

**The fix.** Scrub the page before the policy publishes, and align the guarantee sentence:
> "No. Uptal does not guarantee a job, a job offer, or an interview, and nobody honestly can. What
> Uptal does is keep searching the Saudi market for roles that fit you and applying to them with a CV
> tailored to each one, for as long as your subscription is active."

Hero: "You just take the interviews." becomes "We handle the applying."
Step 3: "Interviews land in your inbox" becomes "Replies land in your inbox", and "You stay hands-free
while the offers come to you" becomes "You stay hands free. Every reply comes straight to you."

### 5. The dead subscriber. Section 15.12.

**Quote:**
> "On notification by Ticket of the death of a subscriber, accompanied by satisfactory evidence, Uptal
> (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) will cancel the Subscription and cease further Charges **with
> effect from the date of that notification**. That cessation is not a refund. Amounts already
> Successfully Charged are treated in accordance with mandatory applicable Saudi law and, subject to
> that law, are **not repaid**."

**The attack.** Three problems, and the middle one is a genuine hole, not a presentation issue.

First: billing continues after death until a bereaved family finds a web form and satisfies Uptal that
the person is dead. "Satisfactory evidence" has no stated standard, and Section 2.2 gives Uptal sole
and absolute discretion over every determination in the document.

Second, and this is the drafting bug: on the policy's own terms the family cannot give that notice at
all. Section 12.6 makes a Ticket the only valid means of giving notice. Section 15.8 says a claim
"may be made only by the Account Holder or by the Payer of Record". Section 15.9 says no third party
has any right under the policy, and expressly names "successor, heir, personal representative". So the
only person entitled to stop the billing is the deceased.

Third: it protects nothing. No merchant has ever improved its position by charging an estate for three
months.

Publishable version: "Uptal keeps charging dead subscribers until their family fills in a web form,
and its own policy says the family has no right to use it."

**Severity: critical.** Highest cruelty-to-value ratio in the document, plus an internal contradiction.

**The fix.** Note that this is not a new refund and does not touch the founder's instruction: an
authority to charge ends at death as a matter of law, so a Charge after death is by definition made
without the authority of the Payer of Record and already falls inside Section 10.2(b). Draft it that
way and it is a correction, not a refund.

> "On being notified of the death of a subscriber, by any means, by a member of the subscriber's family,
> by the subscriber's heirs or personal representatives, or by any person acting for them, Uptal
> (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) will cancel the Subscription and stop all further Charges
> immediately, and will not require the use of the ticketing system for that purpose. A Charge made
> after the date of death is a Charge made without the authority of the Payer of Record within Section
> 10.2(b), and Section 10 applies to it. Sections 12.6, 15.8 and 15.9 do not restrict the giving of a
> notice under this Section."

### 6. The deadline you cannot see. Sections 2.1, 4.4 and 12.2 read together.

**Quote, Section 2.1, definition of "Initiated":**
> "the moment at which **Uptal's billing systems** create the transaction record for that Charge and
> submit it for authorisation, as recorded in the Records"

**Quote, Section 4.4:**
> "the cancellation must be completed and recorded in Uptal's systems **before that Charge is
> Initiated**, and you are advised to complete it not less than twenty four (24) hours before your
> Renewal Date and time as shown in your account."

**Quote, Section 12.2:** the Records are
> "conclusive evidence ... **of the moment of Initiation of each Charge**"

**The attack.** My account shows me a Renewal Date. The policy tells me that date is not the deadline.
The real deadline is an internal event inside Uptal's systems, which can happen at an unstated time
before the date I was shown, and any dispute about when it happened is decided conclusively by the
party that scheduled it. The "advised ... twenty four hours" line is dressed as courtesy but functions
as an undisclosed forfeiture window: it concedes that the date on my screen is wrong by up to a day
without ever saying so.

This is my case exactly. I went to cancel and the charge had already gone, on a date I had been shown
and had planned around. That is not a refund complaint. That is a misleading-practice complaint, and
it survives even if every other clause in the document holds.

**Severity: critical.** The one clause that generates a claim independent of the no-refund rule.

**The fix.** This costs Uptal nothing, because Uptal controls its own scheduler, and it converts the
most contestable clause in the document into the most defensible one. Replace the first paragraph of
Section 4.4 with:

> "Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) does not Initiate a Renewal Charge before the Renewal
> Date and time shown in your Uptal account. A cancellation completed and recorded in Uptal's systems
> before that Renewal Date and time prevents that Renewal Charge. You may cancel at any time up to that
> moment, and your Renewal Date and time are shown in your account at all times."

Keep the existing second and third paragraphs on post-Initiation finality unchanged. The chargeback
file gets stronger too: "the cardholder did not cancel before the date shown in their own account" is
checkable by an issuer. "The cardholder did not cancel before our internal initiation moment" is not.

### 7. The chargeback trap. Sections 11.1, 11.2 and 11.3.

**Quotes, Section 11.1:**
> "you must first submit a Ticket ... and you must allow Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات)
> a reasonable opportunity to review and respond, being **not less than ten (10) Business Days**"
> "Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) **gives no undertaking as to the time within which it
> will respond** substantively to a Ticket. Any delay in, or absence of, a response is not a ground for
> any refund"

**Quote, Section 11.2:**
> "in respect of a Charge that is valid under this Policy and the Terms & Conditions, **or in breach of
> Section 11.1**, is a material breach"

**Quotes, Section 11.3:**
> "(a) suspend, restrict, disable, or terminate your access to the affected product, **to every other
> Uptal product**, and to **every account operated or controlled by you** or by any person acting with
> you or on your behalf"
> "(c) **decline to accept any future order, Subscription, or payment from you**, and decline to permit
> the creation of any further account by you"

**The attack.** Four separate problems, and they compound.

(i) The asymmetry. "Business Day" is defined at 2.1 as excluding Friday and Saturday, so ten Business
Days is about two calendar weeks. I must wait two weeks for a reply the policy expressly does not
promise to give. Meanwhile uptal.com/contact-us says, live, today: "We usually reply within one
business day." Both of those cannot be true and I will publish both.

(ii) 11.2 punishes the person who was right. Filing "in breach of Section 11.1" is a material breach
even where the charge was genuinely invalid. So a customer double-charged by Uptal's own billing
system, who goes to their bank on day three, is in material breach of the contract. That limb is the
one an arbitrator refuses to enforce, and refusing it can take the surrounding clause with it.

(iii) 11.3(a) reaches "every other Uptal product" and "every account operated or controlled by you".
For a job seeker that means losing access to the job search tool because of a dispute over one monthly
charge.

(iv) 11.3(c) is a permanent, lifetime ban from a recruitment platform, unconnected to any outstanding
debt. Against Uptal's stated mission, that is the second best headline in the document after the
got-a-job clause: "Dispute a charge with Uptal and you are barred from ever using it to find work
again."

**Severity: critical.** The contact-first rule is genuinely load-bearing and wins chargebacks. The
trap around it is not.

**The fix.** Keep the defence, remove the trap.

Section 11.1, replace the waiting period and add the missing reciprocity:
> "you must first submit a Ticket at https://uptal.com/contact-us, selecting the 'Billing & payments'
> reason, before contacting your bank, card issuer, payment provider, card scheme, installment or
> wallet provider, or mobile application store, and you must allow Uptal
> (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) a reasonable opportunity to review and respond, being not less
> than five (5) Business Days from the date the Ticket is received. Uptal
> (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) acknowledges every Ticket with a reference on receipt and aims
> to respond to a billing Ticket within one (1) Business Day. Where Uptal has not responded
> substantively within five (5) Business Days, this Section does not prevent you from contacting your
> bank, card issuer, or payment provider."

Section 11.2, delete "or in breach of Section 11.1" and add:
> "Filing a chargeback, payment dispute, or reversal request in respect of a Charge that is not valid
> under this Policy is not a breach of this Policy, whenever it is filed."

Section 11.3(a): delete "and to every account operated or controlled by you or by any person acting
with you or on your behalf", and narrow "to every other Uptal product" to "and to any other Uptal
product in respect of which an amount is outstanding".

Section 11.3(c): replace with
> "(c) decline to accept any future order, Subscription, or payment from you for so long as any amount
> recoverable under paragraph (e) remains unpaid;"

Section 11.4: change "you must promptly withdraw it" to "Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات)
may ask you to withdraw it", keeping the no-false-statements limb intact. A merchant term ordering a
consumer to withdraw a bank dispute is the phrasing regulators like least, and it is not needed: 11.5
already keeps the debt alive.

### 8. Your own staff's word is worthless, and so are their apologies. Sections 4.6 and 15.6.

**Quote, Section 4.6:**
> "Any statement, assurance, **apology**, email, message, ticket reply, chat message, or other
> communication from any such person purporting to have cancelled a Subscription ... **is unauthorised,
> is of no effect, does not bind Uptal**"

Repeated in substance at 6.3, at 6.9 and again at 15.6. Four times.

**The attack.** The anti apparent authority point is genuinely load-bearing and I cannot break it. But
listing *an apology* among the communications that are of no effect is the sentence I read aloud on
air. And saying it four times reads as a company that expects its own support team to lie to customers
and has pre-drafted around it.

The customer-facing half is missing entirely: nowhere does the policy tell me what to do if support
tells me it is handled. It just tells me they were wrong and I lose.

**Severity: critical on framing.** The rule survives. The word "apology" and the fourfold repetition
do not.

**The fix.** State it once in 15.6, cross-refer from 4.6, delete the standalone repeats at 6.3 and 6.9,
delete ", apology," from the list, and give the reader the action:

New plain terms lead to Section 4.6:
> "**In plain terms: cancelling is something only you can do, in your own account settings. Nobody at
> Uptal can do it for you and nobody at Uptal is allowed to say they have. If anyone tells you your
> subscription is cancelled, open your account and check: your Renewal Date and your cancellation
> confirmation are both shown there. If it is still active, cancel it yourself.**"

### 9. One channel, no fallback, and a phone number you tell people not to use.

**Quote, Section 12.6:**
> "**A Ticket is the only valid means of giving Uptal notice under this Policy.** Email to any address,
> social media messages, messages sent through WhatsApp or any other messaging application, **telephone
> calls**, in-product chat, and messages to individual employees ... **do not constitute notice**"

**Quote, live at uptal.com/contact-us:**
> "Call Us. Get in touch directly. +966 50 692 6575. Call Now. Available during business hours"
> "We usually reply within one business day."

**The attack.** Uptal publishes a phone number, puts a "Call Now" button on it, and the policy says the
call counts for nothing. Worse, there is no fallback if the form itself fails. Section 11.1 requires a
Ticket before I go to the bank; Section 12.6 says nothing else counts; so if the form is down, or I am
locked out of the account, or the confirmation email bounces, I am trapped between a mandatory
precondition and an unavailable channel, and going to my bank is then a material breach under 11.2.

I will test that form. If it fails once, I have a screenshot and the strongest paragraph in the piece.
It is also an enforceability risk in its own right: a notice regime with a single privately controlled
channel and no alternative is exactly what a court reads down.

**Severity: high.** Genuine single point of failure with a legal consequence attached to it.

**The fix.** Add to Section 12.6:
> "Where the Uptal ticketing system is unavailable to you, a notice given by any other written means
> that Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) in fact receives is valid notice under this Policy,
> provided that you submit a Ticket recording the same matter as soon as the ticketing system is
> available to you. The burden of establishing the date and content of a notice given by any other
> means rests on you."

And on the contact page, add under the phone number:
> "For anything about a payment, please use the form, so your request is recorded with a reference and
> a time stamp. A phone call cannot be used as your written notice."

And reconcile the reply promise. Either the policy commits to acknowledging a billing Ticket within one
Business Day, per the fix at Finding 7, or the page stops saying it. Publishing both as they stand is
free ammunition.

### 10. The inventory of misfortunes. Sections 6.6, 12.9 and 15.12.

**Quotes:**
> 6.6: "including illness, injury, incapacity, hospitalisation, **imprisonment, military or national
> service, financial hardship**, loss of employment, company restructuring"
> 12.9: "or **any absence, illness, travel, or unavailability** on your part"
> 15.12: "**Illness, injury, incapacity, hospitalisation**, and any other change in a subscriber's health
> or circumstances are not grounds"

**The attack.** These lists do no legal work. The same bullet at 6.6 already says "any change in your
personal, professional, financial, medical, immigration, residency, health, family, or business
circumstances", which covers every named item and anything else. What the named list adds is a
printable inventory of human misfortunes the company has pre-refused: hospital, prison, conscription,
poverty. Every one of them is a caption under a screenshot. Deleting them removes nothing enforceable
and removes four captions.

**Severity: high.** Purely gratuitous.

**The fix.**
6.6: "any change in your personal, professional, financial, or business circumstances, whatever its
nature and however serious;"
12.9: delete "or any absence, illness, travel, or unavailability on your part".
15.12: "A change in a subscriber's health or personal circumstances is not a ground for any refund,
credit, reversal, extension, or compensation."

### 11. Uptal decides whether Uptal did the work. Sections 12.2 and 12.3.

**Quote, Section 12.2:**
> "the Records ... constitute **conclusive evidence** of the fact, amount, currency, time, and
> authorisation of each Charge, ... of the opening of access, of the entitlements and Allowances issued
> to your account, **of the work performed for you**"

**Quote, Section 12.3:**
> "**The absence of a contemporaneous report is evidence that the product was available and functioning**
> during the period in question"

**The attack.** Making a party's own records conclusive proof of the exact fact in dispute is both the
worst-reading clause in the document and the one most likely to be struck. "Uptal's records are
conclusive proof that Uptal performed" writes itself. 12.3 then goes further and deems performance from
my silence, in a document that gives me only one channel through which silence can be broken.

The danger is not only reputational. If a court strikes the conclusive-evidence agreement as a whole
because it overreaches on the contested fact, it takes the uncontroversial machine-generated
transaction facts down with it. The document already contains the correct fallback in the second
paragraph of 12.2. Lead with the split instead of relying on the rescue.

**Severity: high.** Overreach that weakens the clause it is trying to strengthen.

**The fix.** Split conclusive from rebuttable in 12.2:
> "... constitute conclusive evidence of the fact, amount, currency, time, and authorisation of each
> Charge, of the moment of Initiation of each Charge, of the commercial terms disclosed to you at the
> point of sale, of the disclosures and consents presented to and accepted by you, of the version of
> this Policy in force at the time of each Charge, and of the date and content of every cancellation,
> cancellation attempt, pause, Ticket, and other communication, save in the case of manifest error. In
> respect of the opening of access, the entitlements and Allowances issued to your account, and the work
> performed for you, the Records are evidence of those matters unless the contrary is proved."

12.3: change "is evidence that the product was available and functioning" to "may be taken into account
in assessing whether the product was available and functioning".

### 12. Customers may not band together. Sections 15.10 and 15.7.

**Quote, Section 15.10:**
> "any claim you bring ... is to be brought **in your individual capacity only**, and not as a
> representative, collective, consolidated, group, or class claim"

**Quote, Section 15.7:** blocks assignment to, among others,
> "**a law firm acting on any assigned or purchased claim basis**"

**The attack.** The Kingdom has no US-style class action, so 15.10 buys close to nothing and reads as
"customers are forbidden from acting together". 15.7's reference to law firms reads, to a non-lawyer,
as blocking access to representation. Two clauses of near-zero enforcement value and high headline
value, in a document that already has enough of both.

**Severity: high.** Gratuitous.

**The fix.** Delete Section 15.10 in full. In 15.7, keep the block on commercial claim purchasers and
add:
> "Nothing in this Section prevents you from instructing a lawyer or other representative to act for you
> in your own name, or from being represented before any court or competent authority."

### 13. You deleted the score guarantee and added a clause about scores going down. Section 6.2.

**Quote, Section 6.2:**
> "no change, an insufficient change, or **an adverse change** in any score, rating, ranking, evaluation,
> index, or assessment"

**Quote, Section 9.4:**
> "**No score improvement guarantee**, satisfaction guarantee, money back guarantee, or risk free period
> is offered"

**The attack.** These land in the same release, and Section 9.7 announces the deletion of the guarantee
in terms, so they will be read together. The story writes itself: "Uptal used to promise your CV score
would go up or your money back. It has removed that promise, and added a clause saying that if the paid
rewrite makes your score worse, that is not a ground for anything either." The adverse-change limb adds
nothing that "insufficient change" and the Section 9.3 revision remedy do not already cover.

**Severity: high.** One word of drafting, one paragraph of coverage.

**The fix.** Delete ", or an adverse change" from the 6.2 bullet. Add to Section 9.3:

### 14. Thirty days to catch Uptal's own mistake. Sections 10.4 and 12.8.

**Quote, Section 10.4:**
> "in the case of a duplicate charge under Section 10.2(a), **within thirty (30) calendar days** of the
> date of the Charge; and in the case of an unauthorised charge under Section 10.2(b) ... within **one
> hundred and eighty (180) calendar days**"

**Quote, Section 12.8:**
> "Each Charge ... is **treated as correct, accepted, and free from objection** upon the expiry of the
> applicable period"

**The attack.** Uptal gives me 180 days to report a crime committed by a stranger, and 30 days to catch
a mistake made by Uptal's own billing system. On a card I might review quarterly. Against a statement
descriptor the policy itself concedes at 13.6 is a recognition problem. The merchant allows less time
for its own error than for someone else's fraud, and after 30 days deems its own error correct.

And it does not even hold the money. Annex B.3 and the archived block both concede that the issuer
takes it anyway on a duplicate-processing chargeback and that Uptal additionally pays the dispute fee.
So the clause loses money and buys a headline.

**Severity: high.** Costs money, buys nothing, reads as bad faith.

> aware of it and in any event within one hundred and eighty (180) calendar days of the date of the

Then extend the Section 12.7 carve-out to both limbs.

### 15. Everything good in this document is buried. Section 1.2.

**The attack.** Section 1.2 is the only part most people will ever read. It has nine numbered items,
eight of which are refusals, and item 5 reads:
> "**No refund is issued for any Uptal service, on any ground, at any time.**"

Nowhere in that summary does a reader learn any of the following, all of which are true and all of
which are in the document:

- If you were charged and the access or allowance was never actually opened, Uptal opens it or restarts
  the period. Section 5.5.
- If Uptal's records show the cancel control was not working for your account, the cancellation counts
  from your first attempt. Section 4.9.
- A price increase does not touch a subscription you already hold until the new price has been shown to
  you and you have authorised it. Section 13.2. This is more generous than most global SaaS and nobody
  will ever find it.
- Uptal does not charge for a period during the whole of which it has barred you from the product.
  Section 5.6.
- A correction is paid in full, to the original method, and Uptal's costs are never netted off it.
  Sections 10.7 and 11.6.
- The old score improvement guarantee will still be honoured for orders placed before this policy.
  Section 9.7.

Six genuinely good facts, zero surfaced. A reader who finishes the summary has no reason not to
complain publicly, because as far as they can tell nothing whatsoever is available to them. This is
the cheapest fix in the entire document and the highest return.

**Severity: high.** Pure presentation, zero legal cost.

**The fix.** Insert immediately after the plain terms lead of Section 1 and before Section 1.1:

> "**What you can do, in one place.**
> - Cancel any time yourself, at Account, then Billing, then Cancel subscription. Your Renewal Date and
>   time are shown there.

### 16. "Irrevocable", given once, never asked again. Sections 3.8 and 1.4(b).

**Quote, Section 3.8:**
> "This request and this consent are given **once**, at the time of your first purchase or subscription,
> and are standing, continuing, and **irrevocable** in respect of every subsequent Charge ... They are
> **not required to be, and will not be, sought again** at any renewal"

**Quote, Section 1.4(b):** the statutory savings clause
> "does not apply to any right that has been validly affected, reduced, or **extinguished**, including by
> the request and consent you give under Section 3.8"

**The attack.** Item 9 of the plain English summary tells me my unwaivable rights survive. Section 1.4(b)
then tells me those rights are already gone, because of a consent I gave once, at signup, that I can
never withdraw and that will never be sought again. Read together they are a bait and switch, and
"irrevocable, given once, never asked again" is a pull quote.

It is also weaker law than the alternative. A consent asserted to be irrevocable across an indefinite
series of future contracts is exactly what a court reads down. A consent given afresh at each renewal
is close to unattackable, and Section 4.1 already establishes that each renewal is a separate new
purchase, so the fresh consent is already there for the taking.

**Severity: high.** The aggressive version is the weaker version.

**The fix.** Replace the second paragraph of Section 3.8:
> "You give this request and this consent at the time of your first purchase or subscription, and you
> give it again in respect of each subsequent Charge by permitting that Charge to be made, each renewal
> being a separate purchase under Section 4.1. Because it is given afresh in respect of each Charge, it
> is not sought separately again at any renewal, and the fact that it is not sought separately again does
> not affect its validity or its application to that Charge. You may withdraw it for the future at any
> time by cancelling in accordance with Section 4.3, with effect on Charges not yet Initiated."

Section 1.4(b) can then stand unchanged, and is stronger for it.

### 17. The Talent Search page contradicts the policy twice. Sections 7.5 and 7.6.

**Quote, live at uptal.com/talent-search:**
> "How many profile unlocks do I get per month? **Unlimited.**"

**Quote, Policy Section 7.5:**
> "Where your plan includes a **stated Allowance** of profile unlocks ... Unused Allowances do not roll

The live refund policy being replaced says something different again: "100 for Professional, 500 for

**Quote, live at uptal.com/talent-search:**
> "Every profile in the Uptal database goes through a verification process to confirm contact accuracy.

**Quote, Policy Section 7.6:**
> "Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) **does not warrant** that any candidate record is

Minor, but note it while you are in there: that Talent Search page uses em dashes in at least two FAQ

### 18. Retire the product on day eight and keep the year. Section 14.3.

**Quote:**
> "Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) may at any time, and at its **sole discretion**, modify,
> restrict, suspend, discontinue, sunset, retire, withdraw, or replace any product"
> "Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) **may, at its sole discretion**, make available to you a
> substitute or successor Uptal product for the remainder of that Billing Period"

**The attack.** Buy an annual Talent Search plan, Uptal retires the product in week two, keeps the year,
and a substitute is discretionary. It is a clean headline, and it is the clause a Saudi court is most
likely to call arbitrary in an adhesion contract, which under Section 15.1 would then be read down in a
way nobody has chosen.

It also contradicts the document's own architecture. Everywhere else the answer to a failure of service
is re-performance: 5.4, 5.5, 7.6, 8.8, 9.3, 14.2. Here alone the answer is nothing. That inconsistency
is what a court notices.

**Severity: high.** Enforceability risk and headline, both fixable without paying money.

**The fix.** Make the substitute an obligation, keep it non-monetary:
> "Where Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) permanently discontinues a paid product during a
> Billing Period for which you have been Successfully Charged, Uptal
> (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) will make available to you, for the remainder of that Billing
> Period, a substitute or successor Uptal product, or will make the remainder of that Billing Period
> available to you on a substitute or successor Uptal product. That substitution is a re-performance
> governed by Section 5.4, is not a refund, does not entitle you to any choice of product or to any
> comparison of value, and creates no precedent and no entitlement in any other case."

### 19. You wrote that you are selling nothing in particular. Section 3.7.

**Quote:**
> "**No particular feature**, function, interface, design, integration, data source, job source,
> employer, candidate, record, Allowance level, throughput, volume, response time, model, algorithm,
> language, or method of delivery **forms part of the subject matter of any purchase or of the
> consideration for any Charge**"

**The attack.** This goes past disclaiming and denies the contract a determinate object. A Saudi court
applying the Civil Transactions Law needs a defined subject matter; a clause stating that the subject
matter is nothing in particular invites a finding that the object is indeterminate, which would take
down far more than this clause. And for my purposes it is the single best sentence in the document for
the proposition that customers are paying for nothing. The narrower version does all the commercial
work and none of the damage.

**Severity: high.** Overreach that endangers the surrounding architecture.

**The fix.**
> "The subject matter of each purchase is the making available to you, for the relevant Billing Period,
> of the Uptal product concerned as it exists from time to time. No particular feature, function,
> interface, design, integration, data source, Allowance level, throughput, volume, response time, model,
> algorithm, language, or method of delivery is warranted to remain available, to remain unchanged, or to
> be available at any particular level, and the addition, alteration, replacement, reduction, or removal
> of any of them does not constitute a failure of performance, a partial failure of consideration, or a
> breach, and does not affect the finality of any Charge."

### 20. Goodwill, conditional on closing your account. Section 5.7.

**Quote:**
> "may make any such payment or accommodation conditional upon such terms as it thinks fit, including
> the withdrawal in writing of any chargeback, payment dispute, or **complaint**, the provision of a

### 21. The promise that unpromises itself. Section 4.13.

**Quote:**
> "This Section describes what Uptal does. **It confers no right, remedy, or entitlement of any kind**,

**The attack.** Section 4.13 is the only paragraph in nearly thirty thousand words that sounds like a

promise to send you a receipt, and that promise is not a promise." The protection actually needed is
only that a delivery failure does not invalidate a charge, and the following sentence already does that

**Severity: medium.** Gratuitous; the next sentence covers it.

**The fix.** Delete the sentence "It confers no right, remedy, or entitlement of any kind, and it is
not a condition of any Charge or of any cancellation." Keep the rest of the paragraph exactly as it is.

### 22. No delivery date at all, and the remedy for late delivery is delivery. Section 9.5.

**Quote:**
> "**No delivery date, delivery deadline, or turnaround period is given or guaranteed** for an Uptal CV

**The attack.** For a one-off purchase where the fee is earned at the instant of charge, refusing any

your money and we will deliver when we deliver." The Article 14 backstop exists in the next paragraph

hides a fact that would help.

> "Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) aims to deliver an Uptal CV Enhancer order within the

> remedy for delay is delivery."

Keep the Article 14 paragraph unchanged.

### 23. A deadline Uptal sets after the fact, with no minimum. Section 10.5.

**Quote:**

**The attack.** Section 10.2(b) is the only route a fraud victim has. It can be closed by missing a

### 25. The one safe harbour requires Uptal's own logs to convict Uptal. Section 4.9.

**The attack.** The only genuine safe harbour in the document is available only if the company's own

## What actually happens to me, clause by clause. The article in one paragraph.

I got a job on Tuesday. I was charged on Wednesday. I went to cancel and the charge had already gone,
before the date my account showed me, and Section 12.2 makes Uptal's record of that timing conclusive.
I emailed support within the hour: Section 12.6 says my email is not notice. Section 4.5 says it is not
a cancellation. The reply I got: Section 15.6 says it binds nobody, and Section 4.6 says even an apology
is of no effect. The delay in the reply: Section 6.9 says that is not a ground. If I go to my bank
before waiting about two calendar weeks for an answer nobody promised me, Section 11.2 makes me a
material breacher, Section 11.3(a) lets Uptal cut off every Uptal product I use, and Section 11.3(c)
lets Uptal bar me from ever creating an account again. And the reason I no longer need the service, that
I got a job, is named expressly in Section 6.6 as not a ground, "including on the day of, or on any day
after, a Renewal Charge".

Nine clauses. One person. A monthly subscription for unemployed people, sold by a company whose founder
says his mission is to end unemployment.

**Every single one of those nine is fixable above without giving me a riyal.**

## Scorecard: load-bearing versus gratuitous

**Load-bearing, keep as is, I cannot break these.** Section 3 in its entirety, charge equals delivery.
3.9 alternative and cumulative bases. 4.1 renewal as new purchase. 4.2 fixed amount and interval, which
is the correct answer to SAMA Article 90 and better than a reminder. 4.5 what is not a cancellation.
5.1 to 5.3, the substance-not-label rule especially. 5.4 and 5.5, re-performance not money. Section 10
recast as correction and not refund, which is the single best judgment call in the document. 12.1
records. 13.2 refreshed authorisation, which is genuinely more protective than most global SaaS. 15.1 to
15.3 severability and reading down. 15.6 no variation. 11.5 survival of the debt.

**Gratuitous, delete or reframe, costs nothing.** "In the same minute, the same hour, or the same day",
three times. "Including on the day of, or on any day after, a Renewal Charge", twice. The word
"apology" in 4.6. Imprisonment, hospitalisation, military service, financial hardship in 6.6.
"Any absence, illness, travel" in 12.9. "An adverse change" in 6.2. "Whatever its ... duration" in 10.3.
Section 15.10 in full. "The closure of the relevant account" in 5.7. "It confers no right, remedy, or
entitlement of any kind" in 4.13. "Sections 1 to 19 each survive" in 15.13, which is merely absurd.

**Overreach that weakens the thing it protects.** "Conclusive evidence ... of the work performed for
you" in 12.2. "Irrevocable" in 3.8. "No particular feature ... forms part of the subject matter" in 3.7.
"Or in breach of Section 11.1" in 11.2. Sole discretion on the substitute in 14.3. Each of these is
more likely to be read down than the narrower version, and reading down under Section 15.1 puts the
outcome in a judge's hands rather than the founder's.

**The four losses that are not in the policy at all.** The live Auto-Apply privacy FAQ. The live
Auto-Apply "what Uptal does guarantee" answer and the interviews and offers headline copy. The live
Talent Search "Unlimited" unlocks answer. The live Talent Search reachability warranty. Article 10 makes
all four contractual. Fix the marketing in the same release or the policy is publishing into a
contradiction it has already conceded it cannot win.
