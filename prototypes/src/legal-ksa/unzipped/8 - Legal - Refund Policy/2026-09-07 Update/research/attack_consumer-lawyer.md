# ATTACK MEMORANDUM: Uptal Refund Policy (draft of 7 September 2026)

**Prepared for:** the claimant, a subscriber charged on an automatic renewal, seeking recovery of that charge.
**Posture:** contingency. I am paid only if the money comes back. I am therefore not interested in academic criticism of the drafting. I am interested in the shortest route to the money and in the arguments that survive an appeal.
**Counterparty:** Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات), Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, merchant of record.
**Forum options I will use in this order:** (1) the payment rail, under the SAMA Implementing Regulations of the Payments and Payment Services Law, because it is fast, free and the Refund Policy is irrelevant to it; (2) a complaint to the Ministry of Commerce; (3) the competent court in Riyadh.

**One line summary for my client:** this policy is very well drafted and it will beat a lazy claim. It will not beat this one, because it contains three sentences that give the case away, because it makes promises the live website contradicts, and because it has to be read as a whole by a judge holding Article 96 of the Civil Transactions Law.

---

## PART 0. THE FRAME I WILL PUT IN FRONT OF THE JUDGE

Before any individual clause, this is my framing argument, and the drafter should worry about it more than about any single finding below.

Every Uptal purchase is a contract of adhesion. That is not arguable: Article 40 of the Civil Transactions Law defines acceptance in an adhesion contract as mere submission to terms laid down by the offeror which are not open to discussion. A click-through checkout is the paradigm case.

Article 96 then gives the court a power that cannot be drafted around: where an adhesion contract contains arbitrary terms, the court **may amend them or exempt the adhering party from them as justice requires, and any agreement to the contrary is void**. Article 104(3) adds statutory contra proferentem: in an adhesion contract, doubt is construed in favour of the adhering party.

So my case is not "clause 5.1 is unfair". My case is a list, read to the court in one breath. This single document:

1. deems a service delivered whether or not it was in fact delivered, and then says in terms that the rules apply "irrespective of whether any of the matters described in this Section ... in fact occurs in your case" (Section 3.1);
2. makes the merchant's own records conclusive evidence of every fact in dispute (Sections 1.9, 12.2, 12.6);
3. reverses the burden of proof onto the consumer and imposes a heightened evidential standard of "contemporaneous documentary evidence" (Section 12.3);
4. bars any objection to a charge after 30 days and deems the charge "correct, accepted, and free from objection" (Sections 12.7, 12.8);
5. refuses to toll that period while the merchant conducts its own investigation (Section 12.9);
6. reserves every determination to the merchant's "sole and absolute discretion" (Sections 2.2, 10.6, 19);
7. forbids the consumer from assigning the claim to anyone who could fund it (Section 15.7);
8. forbids the consumer from joining any other consumer (Section 15.10);
9. caps liability at the amount of the charge, so that the best possible outcome of litigating is to be put back where you started, minus costs (Section 5.9);
10. makes the exercise of a payment services right a "material breach" carrying forfeiture of the remaining paid period, termination of every product, refusal of future business and recovery of the merchant's costs (Sections 11.2, 11.3);
11. removes every cancellation channel except one control inside an account the consumer may be locked out of (Sections 4.3, 4.5), while naming loss of access to your own email as expressly not a ground for anything (Sections 4.7, 6.6); and
12. states affirmatively that the merchant will send no reminder, no notice and no warning before taking money (Sections 4.3, 4.13, 8.2).

No one of those is fatal. Together they are the picture Article 96 was written for. A judge asked to amend one arbitrary term in that document will not stop at one. **The drafting strategy of "make reading down easy" is sound, but the cumulative density of one sided provisions is now high enough that it defeats its own strategy: it invites wholesale amendment rather than surgical amendment.**

My advice to the other side, if they were paying me, would be to delete four or five of items 1 to 12 entirely. They are worth very little individually and they cost the merchant the tone of the whole document. I identify which ones below.

## PART 1. THE CASE I WOULD ACTUALLY RUN

### The single best argument: the renewal is a new contract by Uptal's own words, and no new contract was validly concluded

This is built entirely out of the merchant's own text. I do not need a single external fact.

**Step 1. Uptal says a renewal is a new contract.** Section 4.1:

> "Each renewal of a Subscription constitutes a separate and distinct purchase of a new Billing Period, on the terms of this Policy and of the Terms & Conditions as in force at the time of that renewal"

Section 1.9 reinforces it: "Each Charge is a separate transaction ... Each renewal ... is a separate Charge governed by the version then published." Section 12.8: "Each Charge stands alone and is separately and finally earned when made."

The drafter chose this characterisation deliberately, to stop my client arguing that the renewal inherits any defect from the original sale, and to make each renewal individually final. It is the load bearing idea of Section 4. I am going to use it against them.

**Step 2. A new contract triggers the mandatory pre-contract statement.** Article 7 of the Implementing Regulations of the E-Commerce Law requires the service provider to provide the statement of terms **before the contract is entered into**, and Article 7(1)(a) requires that statement to say either that a rescission right exists and how to exercise it, **or that no rescission right applies because one of the cases in Article 13(2) of the Law or Article 11 of the Regulations is met**. Article 7(1)(d) and (e) additionally require the contract term, its expiry date, and payment terms **including recurring payments**. Article 7(2) requires the pay button itself to state that clicking it concludes the contract and creates the payment obligation.

**Step 3. Uptal states, in terms, that it discloses nothing before a renewal.** Section 3.8:

> "They are not required to be, and will not be, sought again at any renewal or at any subsequent Charge"

Section 4.3:

> "Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) does not send renewal reminders or pre-renewal notices, is under no obligation to send any renewal reminder or advance notice of any renewal or of any price"

Section 4.13: "Uptal ... does not send renewal reminders or pre-renewal notices and is under no obligation to do so."

**Step 4. The conclusion writes itself.** On Uptal's own characterisation, a new contract for the supply of a service is concluded every month, and not one of the Article 7 pre-contract requirements is satisfied in respect of it. There is no statement of terms, no Article 7(1)(a) statement that no rescission right applies and why, no Article 7(1)(d) or (e) recurring payment disclosure, and no Article 7(2) pay button, because the consumer performs no act at all. Section 1.5's assertion that "this fact is disclosed to you before the contract is concluded, in accordance with Article 7(1)(a)" is true of the first contract only. It is false of the renewal contract, by Uptal's own Section 3.8.

**Step 5. Therefore the Article 13(1) rescission right is live on the renewal contract.** Article 13(1) gives the consumer seven days from the date of contracting for the service to rescind, so long as he has not used the product, has not benefited from the service, and has not obtained any benefit from either. Uptal's defence is that the condition is destroyed at the instant of charge. That defence is factual, and Uptal has disclaimed the facts (see the next argument). And the exemption route under Article 13(2) and Article 11 of the Regulations is unavailable to Uptal on a renewal, because Article 7(1)(a) requires the merchant to have **stated** the applicable exclusion before the contract was concluded, and Uptal has stated nothing.

**Step 6. Section 3.8's answer is a waiver of a public order disclosure duty, and Article 96 voids it.** Section 3.8 says the request and consent are "standing, continuing, and irrevocable". A consumer cannot, in an adhesion contract, waive in advance the merchant's own statutory pre-contract disclosure obligations for an indefinite series of future contracts. That is precisely "an agreement to the contrary" which Article 96 declares void.

**This is what I open with. It wins the renewal charge without any evidence about my client's usage at all.**

### The redraft that defeats it

The founder's commercial objective survives this fix entirely. He wants the renewal charge to be final. He does not need the renewal to be a **new contract**. Those are different things, and the drafter conflated them.

Replace Section 4.1, first paragraph, with:

> **4.1 Every Billing Period is separately earned under one continuing contract**
>
> Your Subscription is a single, continuing contract for the supply of a service of duration, concluded at the time you subscribed, when the statement of terms required by Article 7 of the Implementing Regulations of the Saudi E-Commerce Law was provided to you and accepted by you. A renewal is not a new contract and does not conclude a new contract. It is the continuation of that contract for a further Billing Period, on the same terms, under the same authorisation given under Section 4.2, and on the same standing request for immediate performance given under Section 3.8, and no further statement of terms, disclosure, consent, authorisation, or act by you arises or is required in respect of it.
>
> Each Billing Period is nonetheless a separate and indivisible unit of service with a separate and indivisible price. At the moment a Renewal Charge is Successfully Charged, the service for the renewed Billing Period is delivered in full, performed in full, accepted in full, and irrevocably consumed, and the amount charged is earned in full and becomes final and not refundable, in each case to the maximum extent permitted by applicable Saudi law and in the same way, and to the same extent, as on an initial purchase.

Then make three consequential edits, or the new formulation is contradicted elsewhere:

- **Section 1.9:** replace "Each Charge is a separate transaction, governed by the version of this Policy published on the Uptal website at the time that Charge is made" with "Each Charge is separately earned, and the terms applicable to it are those of the version of this Policy published on the Uptal website at the time that Charge is made, which apply to the continuing contract from that time." Delete the words "Each renewal ... is a separate Charge governed by the version then published" and replace with "The version then published applies to that Charge and to the Billing Period it purchases."
- **Section 4.10:** delete the words "is a separate purchase" and replace with "is a separately earned Charge under the continuing contract, to which Sections 3 and 4.1 apply in full", except for a **reactivation of a cancelled or lapsed Subscription**, which genuinely is a new contract and should be described as one, because there the consumer does perform an act and can be given an Article 7 statement.
- **Section 4.12, final paragraph, and Section 4.11:** delete "that Charge being a new purchase" and "is a Charge to which Sections 3, 4.1 ... apply in full" is retained, but the words "new purchase" must go everywhere they appear in relation to a renewal or an automatic resumption.

Cost to the founder: nothing. Nothing about finality, earning, or non refundability changes. What changes is that Uptal stops volunteering that it concludes an undisclosed contract every month.

### The second best argument: Uptal has disclaimed the facts on which its entire legal theory rests

Section 3.1, final paragraph, is the most damaging sentence in the document, and I will read it to the court twice:

> "The rules established by this Policy apply irrespective of whether any of the matters described in this Section, or in any other descriptive provision of this Policy, in fact occurs in your case, and no assertion that any such matter did not occur affects the finality of any Charge or gives rise to any right, claim, or entitlement."

What the matters described in that Section are: that "your paid access is opened or maintained, the entitlements and Allowances for that Billing Period are issued to your account, and the work that the product comprises begins."

So the merchant has written down, in its own contract, that it does not undertake that access is opened, that Allowances are issued, or that any work begins, and that the customer may not even **assert** that they did not.

This destroys three things at once:

1. **Article 13(1).** The rescission right survives unless the consumer used the service, benefited from it, or obtained a benefit. That is a question of fact. A term that says the merchant's factual recitals need not be true cannot supply the fact. Article 104(3) then resolves the resulting doubt in my client's favour.
2. **Section 3.9, the "alternative and cumulative bases".** Every one of those fallbacks is factual: "performance in fact commenced", "access, features, entitlements, and Allowances were in fact granted, provisioned, and made operative in your account, as evidenced by the Records", "you used the service and obtained a benefit from it". Section 3.1's final paragraph withdraws the merchant's own assertion of each of them.
3. **Section 5.5.** That Section provides a remedy precisely "where the access, entitlements, or Allowances ... were not in fact opened, issued, or made operative in your account". It presupposes that the question can be asked and answered. Section 3.1 says the question is irrelevant and may not even be asserted. The two cannot stand together, and Article 104(3) resolves the conflict for me.

I will also point out that this sentence is what turns a defensible commercial rule into an arbitrary term. A rule that says "we performed, therefore the money is earned" is defensible. A rule that says "the money is earned whether or not we performed" is the definition of تعسفي.

### The redraft that defeats it

**Delete the paragraph.** It buys the merchant nothing that Sections 3.9, 5.5 and 15.1 do not already buy more safely. Replace it with:

> Where, in any individual case, a matter described in this Section did not occur, the remainder of this Policy continues to apply in full, and your remedy in respect of that matter is that provided by Section 5.5, or, where no Uptal service, access, or entitlement of any kind was made available against the Charge at any time, that provided by Sections 1.4 and 15.3. No such matter gives rise to any right to a refund, a credit, or any other remedy described in Section 5.2.

That keeps the money, keeps the no refund rule, keeps re-performance as the sole remedy, and removes the one sentence I would have built my cross examination around.

### The third best argument: the payment rail, where this Policy has no application at all

SAMA's Implementing Regulations of the Payments and Payment Services Law, Article 90, entitles the payer to a refund **from the payee's payment service provider** of the full amount of an authorised, payee initiated payment transaction where both (a) the authorisation did not specify the exact amount when consent was given, and (b) the amount exceeds what the payer could reasonably have expected. Article 91(1) gives eight weeks from the debit date, and Article 91(3) requires the payee's PSP to refund or justify refusal within ten business days.

Three things matter about this route:

- It runs against **Uptal's acquirer**, not against Uptal. My client is not a party to any contract with that acquirer, and neither the Refund Policy nor Uptal's Section 15.9 "no third party rights" clause has anything to say about it. Section 11.2 purports to make my use of it a "material breach". I will advise my client to use it anyway and I will tell the court that a term penalising the exercise of a statutory payment services right is arbitrary. See Finding H7.
- It does not care whether the service was delivered.
- It resolves in ten business days.

Uptal's defence is Section 4.2:

> "The authority you give is an authority to charge a specific stated amount at a specific stated interval. The amount and the interval are displayed to you at checkout, are accepted by you before payment, are recorded in the confirmation of your purchase, are shown in your Uptal account at all times, and are recorded in the Records."

That is a **recital of fact**, not a term. I will demand the checkout artefact: the exact screen, the exact amount, the exact interval, the timestamp, and the affirmative acceptance. If it does not exist in that form, limb (a) of Article 90 is open and the money comes back regardless of everything else in the document.

And the drafter has left **two doors open even if the checkout artefact is perfect**. Both are self inflicted:

**Door 1: the automatic resumption after a pause.** Section 4.12:

> "A paused Subscription resumes automatically at the end of the pause unless you have cancelled it in accordance with Section 4.3 before then, and by pausing you authorise in advance the Charge that falls due on resumption, at the price displayed to you at the time of that resumption"

Read that carefully. A subscriber who has paused, by definition, has stepped away. Months later the Subscription resumes automatically and is charged at "the price displayed to you at the time of that resumption", which is a price the subscriber has never seen and could not have seen, because they were not there. **Limb (a) is met: the authorisation, given when the pause was set, did not specify the exact amount. Limb (b) is met if the price has moved.** Section 13.2's protection does not save this, because 13.2 lists "a resumption after a pause" as a refreshing event that resets the authorised amount, rather than as an occasion requiring fresh consent. Uptal has drafted the Article 90 fact pattern into its own policy.

**Door 2: the step up after a promotional price.** Section 4.11:

> "A promotional or introductory price applies only for the period disclosed, after which the price then displayed to you applies at each renewal and becomes the authorised amount for the purposes of Sections 4.2 and 13.2."

Note the tense: it "becomes" the authorised amount. That is a concession that it was not the authorised amount when consent was given. Section 4.11's disclosure obligation is expressly confined to the case "Where a free or reduced period is followed by a paid period", which covers a trial converting to a paid plan. It does **not** cover the far commoner case of a promotional or introductory **price** for a stated number of periods, followed by the list price. For every customer acquired on an introductory price, limb (a) of Article 90 is met at the step up, and limb (b) is met by definition, because the amount is higher than the amount the payer had been paying. A contractual deeming cannot bind the payer's own bank or Uptal's acquirer.

### The redrafts that defeat it

**Section 4.12, final paragraph, replace with:**

> A paused Subscription resumes automatically at the end of the pause unless you have cancelled it in accordance with Section 4.3 before then. The Charge that falls due on resumption is made in the amount last authorised by you for your plan under Section 4.2, and by pausing you authorise that Charge in advance. Where the price of your plan has changed during the pause, the Subscription does not resume, and no Charge is made, until the new amount and interval have been displayed to you and accepted by you as a refreshed authorisation under Section 13.2. Until that acceptance is given, the Subscription remains paused and no Charge is made.

**Section 4.11, replace the final sentence with:**

> Where a Charge is to be made at a promotional, introductory, or discounted amount for a stated number of Billing Periods, the amount that will be charged at each subsequent renewal, and the Billing Period from which it will apply, are displayed to you and accepted by you before you provide your payment details, and it is that amount which you authorise under Section 4.2 for those subsequent renewals. Where they are not so displayed and accepted, each renewal is charged in the amount last authorised by you, and the higher amount applies only from the point at which it has been displayed to you and incorporated into a refreshed authorisation under Section 13.2.

**Section 13.2, add to the list of refreshing events, and qualify it:**

> A refreshed authorisation is given only where the new recurring amount and the interval are displayed to you as the amount and interval that will apply to your future renewals, and are accepted by you by a separate affirmative act at the time. The automatic updating, re-issue, or replacement of card credentials by you, by your card issuer, or by an account updater service is not a refreshed authorisation, and the correction of declined payment details is a refreshed authorisation only where the new amount is displayed and separately accepted at that time.

Cost to the founder: nothing on published pricing, nothing on new purchases, and nothing on the no refund rule. What it removes is a route by which the bank simply takes the money out of Uptal's account with no defence available.

### C5. CRITICAL. Section 15.3, third paragraph. An irrebuttable contractual valuation of a statutory restitution award.

Quote:

> "the value of the access, availability, Allowances, and performance in fact provided is **deemed to be the whole of the price** of the Billing Period or order concerned wherever access was opened, an Allowance was issued, or a deliverable was produced, irrespective of the extent, quantity, quality, or existence of any output"

**The attack.** Section 15.3 is the clause the founder is relying on to survive a strike out: it is the "if the rule falls, here is how little we pay" clause. As drafted it does the opposite. It tells the court, in advance, how to calculate an award that the court is being required to make **by a mandatory provision of Saudi law**. Article 96 voids "any agreement to the contrary" of the court's power to amend arbitrary terms; a clause that sets the amount of the remedy to exactly zero in every case is an agreement to the contrary. Articles 144, 145 and 148 of the Civil Transactions Law require a person who has received a payment not due to return it, and Article 145 in terms: whoever receives by way of payment what is not due to him must return it. A contractual deeming that the value received always equals the whole price is a device to defeat that obligation in every case.

Note the words "irrespective of ... the existence of any output". The merchant has written that the value provided is the whole price even where no output existed. That single phrase is why this clause will be struck rather than read down, and its striking reopens the quantum question completely, which is a far worse outcome for Uptal than a modest presumption.

**The redraft.** Make it a rebuttable presumption. A rebuttable presumption survives Article 96; an irrebuttable one invites the court to take the pen.

> For the purposes of paragraph (iii), the value of the access, availability, Allowances, and performance in fact provided to the person concerned is to be taken into account in full, and, in the absence of evidence to the contrary, is to be taken to be the whole of the price of the Billing Period or order concerned where access was opened, an Allowance was issued, or a deliverable was produced, irrespective of the extent to which the person concerned in fact used what was made available. Where it is established that no access was opened, no Allowance was issued, and no deliverable was produced against the Charge, this paragraph does not apply and Section 5.5 governs.

### C6. CRITICAL. Section 2.1, definition of "Initiated", with Section 4.4. A cut-off the consumer cannot see and the merchant does not cap.

Quote, from Section 2.1:

> "**'Initiated'** means, in relation to a Charge, the moment at which Uptal's billing systems create the transaction record for that Charge and submit it for authorisation, as recorded in the Records"

and from Section 4.4:

> "A Renewal Charge that has been Initiated is valid, is fully earned once it is Successfully Charged, and is unaffected by any cancellation completed or recorded after the moment of Initiation"

**The attack.** The operative deadline for the single most important act a subscriber can perform is defined by reference to an internal event inside the merchant's systems, invisible to the subscriber, evidenced only by the merchant's own records, which Section 12.2 makes conclusive. Nothing in the document caps how far before the displayed Renewal Date Initiation may occur. On the text as drafted, Uptal could Initiate a renewal a week early, while the subscriber's account still displays a future Renewal Date, and every cancellation in that week would be too late. Section 4.4's "you are advised to complete it not less than twenty four (24) hours before your Renewal Date" is expressly advisory and confers nothing.

Set that against what Uptal advertises. Every relevant page says **"Cancel anytime"**. The Auto-Apply FAQ says **"cancel at any time with no commitment. You're never locked in."** The Talent Search pricing block says **"No setup fees. No recruiter commissions. Cancel anytime."** Under Article 10 of the E-Commerce Law an electronic advertisement is a contractual document supplementing the contract and binding on the parties. Section 6.9 tries to pre-empt this by saying such statements "refer to your ability to prevent future Charges and never to the reversal of a Charge already made", but that is exactly my point: my client's ability to prevent a future charge was defeated by a deadline she could not see.

Article 104(3) resolves the ambiguity between "cancel anytime" and an invisible cut-off in my client's favour, and Article 96 makes an unobservable, uncapped, merchant defined deadline a textbook arbitrary term.

**The redraft.** Cap it. This costs Uptal nothing operationally and removes an entire category of dispute.

Add to Section 4.4, as a new first paragraph:

> A Renewal Charge is not Initiated earlier than twenty four (24) hours before the Renewal Date and time shown in your Uptal account. A cancellation completed by you and recorded in Uptal's systems before that time prevents that Renewal Charge. Where a Renewal Charge is Initiated earlier than that time, a cancellation completed by you and recorded in Uptal's systems before the Renewal Date shown in your account is treated as having been completed before Initiation, and the Billing Period created by that Charge is treated in accordance with Section 4.9.

And amend the advisory sentence so it reads as guidance rather than as a trap:

> "To prevent the next Renewal Charge, complete your cancellation before the Renewal Date and time shown in your account. Uptal recommends that you complete it not less than twenty four (24) hours beforehand."

### C7. CRITICAL. The live advertising contradicts the Policy on five specific points, and Article 10 makes the advertising contractual.

Quote from Section 1.7:

> "Nothing in this Section purports to exclude or limit the effect of Article 10 of the Saudi E-Commerce Law, under which an electronic advertisement is a contractual document supplementing the contract; where any electronic advertisement forms part of your contract by operation of that Article, it is to be read together with, and consistently with, this Policy and with Sections 8.5, 8.6 and 9.4."

**The attack.** "Read together with, and consistently with" is not a conflict rule. It does not say which prevails. So where the advertisement and the Policy conflict, there is no contractual resolution, and Article 104(3) resolves it in my client's favour. The drafter has, in a single sentence, conceded that Article 10 applies and then failed to say what happens when it bites. These are the live conflicts as at today, taken from Uptal's own published pages:

**(a) Pausing.** Auto-Apply FAQ: **"Pause whenever you need a break and pick up right where you left off, or cancel at any time with no commitment. You're never locked in."**
Policy Section 4.12 and Section 8.7: pausing "does not shorten, extend, suspend, bank, preserve, transfer, credit, or carry forward the Billing Period then current or any unused part of it, **which expires and is forfeited**."
"Pick up right where you left off" is an express advertised promise that the unused remainder is preserved. It is the precise opposite of forfeiture. On a pause case I win this outright.

**(b) The performance guarantee Uptal actually gives.** Auto-Apply FAQ: **"Does Uptal guarantee a job? No. Uptal does not guarantee a job, a job offer, or an interview. What Uptal does guarantee is that it keeps searching for suitable roles and applying to them with a CV tailored to each one."**
Policy Section 8.4 lists as not a ground: "the number of roles matched, or applications tailored or submitted, during the period, **including where none is matched, tailored, or submitted**."
Uptal has advertised a guarantee of continuous searching and applying and has then written a policy saying that a period in which nothing was searched or applied for gives rise to no remedy. Article 10 makes the guarantee a contract term. Article 107 of the Civil Transactions Law gives the customer performance or rescission plus compensation on non-performance. Section 3.3 cannot beat a contractual guarantee of a specified activity.

**(c) Unlimited unlocks.** Talent Search FAQ item 7: **"How many profile unlocks do I get per month? Unlimited."**
Policy Section 7.5 is built on "a stated Allowance of profile unlocks", forfeiture of unused Allowances, and Section 12.2 making the Records conclusive evidence of "the level of any Allowance included in the service you purchased". If the advertisement says unlimited, an Allowance cap is a term the customer never agreed to, and the conclusive evidence clause is being used to overwrite an advertised contractual term. Section 1.6 ("point of sale terms prevail on commercial detail") does not help: an FAQ on the product page is part of the point of sale and is, by statute, a contractual document.

**(d) Verified and reachable candidates.** Talent Search FAQ item 4: **"Every profile in the Uptal database goes through a verification process to confirm contact accuracy. Verified profiles include confirmed email addresses and phone numbers, so when you unlock a profile and reach out, you're contacting real, reachable candidates, not outdated records."** The pricing card repeats it: "Verified email & phone contact info", "Full access to 1M+ verified candidates".
Policy Section 7.6: **"Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) does not warrant that any candidate record is accurate, current, or complete, or that any candidate is reachable, available, responsive, or suitable."**
That is a direct contradiction between an advertised warranty and a contractual disclaimer of the same warranty. Article 10 makes the advertisement binding. Section 7.6 then caps my remedy at re-verification within thirty days of the unlock, which is a limitation of a remedy for breach of an advertised warranty, in an adhesion contract, and Article 96 applies.

**(e) The advertised response time and the advertised phone line.** See Finding H5.

**The redraft.** Two parts, and the second is not optional.

First, give Section 1.7 an actual conflict rule:

> Where an electronic advertisement forms part of your contract by operation of Article 10 of the Saudi E-Commerce Law and its terms differ from this Policy, the advertisement governs the description of the service to be supplied, and this Policy governs the financial consequences of a Charge, including the delivery, performance, and acceptance of the service, cancellation, renewals, refunds, corrections of Charges, chargebacks, and payment disputes. ### C8. CRITICAL. The locked out subscriber. Section 4.3 removes every channel, Section 4.9 does not reach the case, and Sections 4.7 and 6.6 make it the customer's fault.

Quotes. Section 4.3: **"Cancellation of a Subscription is performed by the subscriber, personally, using the self-service cancellation function in the subscriber's own Uptal account settings, and that is the only means of cancelling a Subscription."**
Section 4.7 lists as not a ground: **"loss of access to your email account, loss of access to your payment method"**.

Section 4.9 covers only the case where **"Uptal's Records show that the self-service cancellation function was unavailable to your account, or failed when operated by you"**.

**The attack.** Uptal has abolished every other cancellation route, made a support ticket expressly inert (Section 4.5), forbidden its own staff from helping (Section 4.6), and then declared that if the subscriber cannot get into the account, that is the subscriber's problem and billing continues indefinitely. That is the finding a Ministry of Commerce inspector will make in ten minutes. The Ministry's E-Store Guide requires a clear and easy consumer account cancellation control. An illusory remedy is worse than no remedy: it is evidence of arbitrariness.

**The redraft.** Extend Section 4.9. This costs no refund, because the remedy is prospective only, and it removes the single most dangerous fact pattern in the document.

Add to Section 4.9, after the first paragraph:

> This Section applies in the same way where you were unable to reach or to operate the self-service cancellation function because you were unable to access your Uptal account, and you notified Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) of that fact by a Ticket submitted at https://uptal.com/contact-us. It is not a refund, is not a Correction under Section 10, and is not an exception to Section 5.1. ### H1. Section 12.8. "Treated as correct, accepted, and free from objection" is a contractual limitation period by another name.

Quote: **"Each Charge, and each tax invoice issued in respect of it, is treated as correct, accepted, and free from objection upon the expiry of the applicable period in Section 12.7 without a compliant Ticket having been submitted"**

Section 5.3 of this very Policy tells me how to characterise this: **"This Section 5 applies according to the substance and effect of what is sought, and not according to the description, label, or characterisation applied to it."** I will apply the merchant's own principle to the merchant's own clause.

> Compliance with Section 12.7 is a condition of Uptal's internal review process. ### H7. Sections 11.2, 11.3 and 11.5. Punishing the exercise of a statutory payment right, punishing before any determination, and punishing the customer who wins.

Quotes. Section 11.2: **"The filing of a chargeback, payment dispute, reversal request, or claim of non-receipt or unauthorised use ... in respect of a Charge that is valid under this Policy and the Terms & Conditions, or in breach of Section 11.1, is a material breach of this Policy and of the Terms & Conditions."**

Quote, Section 2.2 and repeated verbatim in Section 19: **"Where Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) has a right of determination, verification, approval, or discretion under this Policy, that right may, to the maximum extent permitted by applicable Saudi law, be exercised in Uptal's sole and absolute discretion."**

**The attack.** There is no floor on the period Uptal may state. ### H10. Section 1.10. Existing subscribers are deemed, by silence and with no notice, to have given a fresh Article 13 consent.

Quote: **"Where you subscribed before that date, your holding of the Subscription on or after that date, and your permitting of the first and every subsequent renewal of it on or after that date, constitute your acceptance of this Policy and your giving of the request and consent set out in Section 3.8 in respect of that and every subsequent Charge."**

### H13. Section 5.2 excludes restitution and unjust enrichment outright, and Section 1.4(d) tries to narrow a mandatory provision.

Quotes. **The attack.** Articles 144, 145 and 148 of the Civil Transactions Law impose an obligation to return a payment received that was not due. Article 145 in terms: whoever receives by way of payment what is not due to him must return it. ### H14. Section 9.5. A one time purchase with no delivery date at all, and delivery as the only remedy for never delivering.

### H15. Section 1.9 asserts conclusive evidence with no rebuttal fallback, while Sections 12.2 and 12.6 have one.

### H16. Section 12.3 reverses the burden of proof and imposes a heightened evidential standard by contract.

### H19. Section 7.2. Asking a Talent Search subscriber to warrant a legal characterisation the statute does not permit them to waive.

**The attack.** Ten Business Days, with Friday and Saturday excluded and Saudi public holidays excluded, is a minimum of fourteen calendar days and routinely more. Around Eid it can be twenty five. So the two limbs of the same sentence contradict one another for most of the year. Article 104(3) means the customer takes the shorter one, which is fine for the customer and embarrassing for the merchant, and the drafter should note that the Ministry has consulted on a fourteen day refund turnaround requirement, so fourteen calendar days is the number that will matter.

**The redraft.**

> An approved Correction is paid to the original payment method only, to the Payer of Record only, in the original currency of the Charge, in full and without any deduction, netting, set-off, or fee of any kind, and within fourteen (14) calendar days of approval, subject to the processing times of the relevant payment provider, for which Uptal (شركة الخبرات الصاعدة لتقنية المعلومات) is not responsible.

### M8. Section 12.7's thirty day general window is shorter than every statutory route and its only practical effect is to send customers to their banks.

Quote: **"must be raised by you in writing by Ticket at https://uptal.com/contact-us within thirty (30) calendar days of the date of the Charge concerned"**

**The attack.** SAMA Article 89(8) gives a payment service user up to six months from the debit date to notify an unauthorised transaction. Articles 90 and 91 give eight weeks for the payee-PSP refund route. A thirty day contractual window for the general case is shorter than both, cannot bar either, and therefore achieves nothing except to tell a customer on day thirty five that Uptal will not look at her complaint, which is the sentence that produces a chargeback. Every such chargeback lands in Uptal's dispute ratio and its scheme monitoring programme numerators.

**The redraft.** Make the general window match the longstop, and keep the shorter period as a request rather than a bar.

> Any claim, query, objection, or dispute of any kind relating to a Charge ... should be raised by you in writing by Ticket at https://uptal.com/contact-us as soon as possible and in any event within one hundred and eighty (180) calendar days of the date of the Charge concerned, save that the longer period in Section 10.4 applies to a request relating to an unauthorised charge under Section 10.2(b). Raising a matter within thirty (30) calendar days of the Charge, or of the date on which you first became aware of the matter complained of, materially improves Uptal's ability to investigate it, and a delay beyond that period is a matter to be taken into account in assessing the evidence available.

### M9. Section 1.2 item 5 tells the customer something items 7 and 9 qualify, in a summary the Policy makes contractual.

Quote, item 5: **"No refund is issued for any Uptal service, on any ground, at any time."**
Item 7 then describes two corrections; item 9 preserves mandatory rights.

**The attack.** Section 1.3 makes the summary part of the Policy. Article 104(3) construes it against the drafter. The MoC E-Store Guide forbids unclear or misleading presentation of terms. A summary whose fifth line states an absolute that its own seventh and ninth lines qualify is the definition of unclear.

**The redraft.** Item 5:

> 5. No refund is issued under this Policy for any Uptal service, on any ground, at any time. There is also no credit, voucher, discount, extension, free period, set-off, compensation, or goodwill gesture in place of one. Section 5. Two billing events that do not pay for any service are dealt with separately in Section 10, and your unwaivable rights under Saudi law are preserved by Section 1.4.

### M10. Section 13.6 still carries a bracketed editorial note.

Quote: **"the Uptal statement descriptor, **UPTAL** [PRE-PUBLICATION NOTE: insert here, character for character, the exact statement descriptor string registered with Uptal's acquirer, and delete this note before publication]"**

**The attack.** If this ships, it is the first thing I screenshot. A legal instrument published with an internal editorial instruction visible tells a court and a regulator exactly how much care went into the rest of it, and it undermines every "the Records are conclusive" assertion in the document. It also leaves the descriptor unstated, which is the single commonest cause of "I do not recognise this charge" disputes against a merchant whose legal name is an Arabic string.

**The redraft.** Insert the registered descriptor string, character for character, and delete the note. If it is not available before publication, delete the whole sentence rather than publish the note.

## PART 3. WHAT I WOULD DO IF I WERE DEFENDING THIS

Because the founder's objective is achievable, and because I want the record to show that I distinguished the clauses that will hold from the clauses that will not.

**What holds, and holds well:**

- The Article 13(1) use and benefit theory, provided Section 3.1's final paragraph is deleted and the delivery events are actually logged.
- The CV Enhancer position under Article 13(2)(a) and Article 11(e) of the Regulations. It is the strongest statutory ground in the whole document and Section 9.1 states it correctly.
- Section 5.4, re-performance is not a refund. Correct in principle and correctly drafted.
- Section 10, the recast of the two corrections as not being refunds. This is legally right, not merely commercially clever, and it is what allows the absolute in Section 5.1 to stand at all.
- Section 15.1 and 15.2, the reading down order. Genuinely well drafted and it will work.
- The exhaustive non-grounds list in Section 6. Counterintuitively this is the strongest consumer facing part of the document, because Article 104(3) punishes ambiguity and specificity removes it.
- Section 4.8, cancel at any time with access retained to the end of the paid period. This is fair, it is what the advertising says, and it removes a whole category of grievance.

**What I would delete outright before publishing, because each costs more than it earns:**

1. Section 3.1, final paragraph. The single most damaging sentence in the document.
2. Section 12.3, the sentence beginning "The absence of a contemporaneous report is evidence that".
3. Section 12.8, the sentence deeming a Charge "correct, accepted, and free from objection".
4. Section 15.10, the collective claims prohibition.
5. The word "forfeited", everywhere. Replace with "not repaid".
6. Section 15.3's irrebuttable deeming. Make it rebuttable.

**The five operational facts that decide every case, whatever the drafting:**

1. Does the checkout display and record the exact recurring amount and interval, with an affirmative acceptance? If not, SAMA Article 90 is open on every renewal and no clause helps.
2. Does every paid period produce a logged delivery event? If not, Article 13(1) is open and Section 3.9's fallbacks are all factual.
3. Does the cancel control exist at exactly the path the Policy names, and is every attempt, including every failed attempt, logged? If not, Section 4.9 is a promise Uptal cannot audit.
4. Does account recovery work? If not, Finding C8 is a Ministry complaint waiting to be filed.
5. Do the three messages in Section 4.13 actually go out? The cancellation confirmation is the cheapest and most valuable artefact in the entire dispute stack.

**And the one that is not about drafting at all:** fix the five advertising claims in Finding C7 before this Policy publishes. Article 10 of the E-Commerce Law makes them contract terms. No refund policy, however well drafted, can outrun an advertisement that says "pick up right where you left off", "unlimited", "reachable", or "what Uptal does guarantee".
