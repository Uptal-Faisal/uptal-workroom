# IE market pack: sources and reasoning

Written 2026-10-12. **No live research was possible in this run.** The shared web-search budget for the turn was already used up, and the proxy blocked direct fetches of the Irish official and payment hosts (citizensinformation.ie, enterprise.gov.ie, revenue.ie, docs.stripe.com). Every fact below therefore comes from prior knowledge, which runs to mid-2026. Each item has a confidence level and the canonical page to re-check. Where I was unsure, I took the conservative option. Items marked **VERIFY** should be checked before launch.

## Payments
- **Cards first, then PayPal; Apple Pay and Google Pay as wallets.** Irish online checkout is card-led. Most Irish bank debit cards are Visa Debit or Debit Mastercard, and they work online. The sub-label says "debit or credit" because many users pay with a debit card. Revolut has a very large Irish user base, but its cards are Visa or Mastercard, so the card option covers it. Apple Pay is widely used and supported by every main Irish bank (AIB, Bank of Ireland, PTSB) and by Revolut, and Google Pay is broadly supported too. Samsung Pay/Wallet payments are not a standard checkout option in Ireland, so we left them out (conservative). Confidence: high.
  - Re-check: https://www.bankingandpayments.ie (Banking & Payments Federation Ireland payment statistics) and https://www.centralbank.ie/statistics/data-and-analysis/credit-and-banking-statistics/payment-statistics
- **No local method ahead of cards.** Ireland has no dominant account-to-account scheme like iDEAL, Bancontact or BLIK. Open-banking "pay by bank" exists through PSPs but is not mainstream for subscriptions. SEPA Direct Debit is common for utilities and gyms, not for app subscriptions. Confidence: high.
- **localCardBrand: null.** Ireland's domestic debit scheme, Laser, closed in 2014, and banks moved to Visa Debit and Debit Mastercard. Confidence: high.
- **BNPL: Klarna Pay in 3, with a caveat. VERIFY.** Klarna serves Irish consumers (klarna.com/ie) with interest-free Pay in 3, and the major PSPs list Ireland as a Klarna market. BNPL use is lower in Ireland than in the UK, so this is a judgement call. Since 2022, BNPL providers in Ireland have fallen under Central Bank of Ireland regulation. The EU Consumer Credit Directive 2 (Directive (EU) 2023/2225) applies from 20 Nov 2026 and brings interest-free BNPL into full consumer-credit rules, including creditworthiness checks and disclosures, so Klarna's checkout flow and copy may change. For subscriptions, saved Klarna methods charged without the customer present usually cannot use instalments, so we kept the GB wording ("Renewals are charged in full"). Before launch, confirm with the PSP that Klarna Pay in 3 is enabled for IE merchants and for recurring plans. If not, set `bnpl` to `[]`.
  - Re-check: https://www.klarna.com/ie/ and https://docs.stripe.com/payments/klarna (country / payment-option table)
  - https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2225/oj (CCD2)

## Tax
- **VAT at 23%, included in the price.** 23% is Ireland's standard VAT rate (restored on 1 Mar 2021 after the temporary 21% COVID cut), and B2C electronically supplied services are standard-rated. EU and Irish price-indication rules require consumer prices to include VAT, so `incl: true`. Budget 2026 (Oct 2025) cut VAT on food and catering/hospitality to 9% from 1 Jul 2026, which does not affect us. Budget 2027 was due in early October 2026, after the cut-off of my knowledge. A change to the standard rate would be very unusual, but **VERIFY**. A non-EU supplier selling to Irish consumers charges Irish VAT through the EU One-Stop Shop (non-Union scheme). Confidence: high on 23%.
  - Re-check: https://www.revenue.ie/en/vat/vat-rates/search-vat-rates/current-vat-rates.aspx

## Work rights (`rights`)
- **Auto-answered: Irish, British, EU/EEA and Swiss citizens.** British citizens may live and work in Ireland without permission under the Common Travel Area (CTA), confirmed after Brexit by the 2019 UK-Ireland CTA memorandum. EU/EEA nationals have free movement, and Swiss nationals have the same rights through the EU-Swiss free movement agreement. The `autoFor` list is the 27 EU states plus GB, IS, LI, NO and CH, the same EU/EEA/CH set as the NL pack, with GB added. Confidence: high.
  - https://www.irishimmigration.ie/my-situation-has-changed-since-i-arrived-in-ireland/common-travel-area/ (CTA)
- **Six options, the last one needing a permit:**
  1. Irish or British citizen (CTA).
  2. EU/EEA or Swiss citizen.
  3. Stamp 4, plus the other permissions that let you work for any employer without a permit: Stamp 4 EUFam (family of an EU citizen), Stamp 5 and Stamp 6. Beneficiaries of EU temporary protection (mainly Ukrainians, extended to March 2027) can also work without a permit, so they are named in the sub-label. Confidence: high (temporary protection end date: medium).
  4. Stamp 1G. The Third Level Graduate Programme (12 months after a level 8 degree, 24 months after a level 9) and spouses or de facto partners of Critical Skills Employment Permit holders (since 2019) can work full-time without a permit. In 2024 the right to work was also extended to spouses and partners of General Employment Permit holders, but I could not re-confirm the details, so the sub-label names only Critical Skills (conservative). Confidence: high for graduates and CSEP spouses.
  5. Current Critical Skills or General Employment Permit holder. Permits are tied to the employer, so a move needs a new permit. Under the Employment Permits Act 2024 (commenced Sept 2024), holders can normally change employer after 9 months instead of 12. That number is left out of the copy because I could not re-verify it. CSEP holders can usually move to Stamp 4 after 2 years. Confidence: high on "new employer needs a new permit".
  6. Needs a permit. In Ireland this is formally an "employment permit", which the employer or the employee can apply for with a job offer. Irish job ads still say "visa sponsorship", so the sub-label bridges the two terms.
  - Re-check: https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/ and https://www.irishimmigration.ie/registering-your-immigration-permission/information-on-registering/immigration-permission-stamps/
- **Context for the matching rule (salary minimum). VERIFY.** The Critical Skills Employment Permit had a minimum of EUR 38,000 for degree-level roles on the Critical Skills Occupations List and EUR 64,000 for any eligible occupation from 1 Jan 2024. The General Employment Permit minimum was EUR 34,000 (EUR 30,000 for graduates in the first year). The Employment Permits Act 2024 set annual reviews, and higher thresholds were scheduled for 2025-26. I could not confirm the current figures, so the user-facing rule says "the permit salary minimum" without a number. An 8-year analyst on about EUR 72k clears the 2024 CSEP minimum for any eligible occupation, and if the 2026 figure is higher, a GEP is the fallback. I could not confirm whether finance and investment analysts (SOC 2422) are on the Critical Skills list. The rule wording ("skip jobs that say they can't sponsor or that pay below the permit minimum") is therefore safe either way.

## Nationalisation
- **"EEA hiring priority" is filled in, not null.** Ireland has no citizen quota like Emiratisation, but its permit system has two EEA-first rules of the same kind as the spec's "Egypt's foreign-worker quota" example and the MA/TN national-preference entries:
  - The **50:50 rule**: when the application is made, at least 50% of the employer's workforce must be EEA nationals. A waiver exists for start-ups supported by Enterprise Ireland or IDA in their first two years. It has been in force since the Employment Permits (Amendment) Act 2014 and, as far as I know, was kept by the 2024 Act.
  - The **labour market needs test** for General Employment Permits: the role must first be advertised to EEA jobseekers, for 28 days on the Department of Social Protection's Jobs Ireland/EURES. Critical Skills permits are exempt.
  - Fact-check pass (2026-10-12): the copy now says "most employers" and "usually", because the 50:50 rule can be waived for Enterprise Ireland/IDA-supported start-ups and the labour market needs test has exemptions. Live checks were again impossible (search budget used up; proxy denies revenue.ie, enterprise.gov.ie, docs.stripe.com).
  - Confidence: high that both rules exist, medium on the exact 2026 wording of the advertising requirement. The copy says "advertised to EEA jobseekers for 28 days" without naming the platform, and states plainly that none of this applies to people who don't need a permit (Ciarán, our persona). If the product wants a strictly citizen-quota definition, set this to `null` like GB/NL/BE.

## Sensitive fields
- **photo avoid, gender/dob/nationality/marital none.** The Employment Equality Acts 1998-2015 ban employment discrimination, including at recruitment, on nine grounds: gender, civil status, family status, sexual orientation, religion, age, disability, race (including nationality and ethnic origin) and membership of the Traveller community. Claims go to the Workplace Relations Commission. Irish CV advice (Citizens Information, recruiters, gradireland) is to leave out photo, date of birth and marital status. Irish forms do ask about the right to work, meaning whether you need a permit or sponsorship and your stamp, and the rights card covers that. Confidence: high.
  - https://www.ihrec.ie/your-rights/i-am-an-employee/ and https://www.workplacerelations.ie/en/what_you_should_know/equal_status_employment_equality/

## Legal
- **GDPR + Data Protection Act 2018 (Ireland).** The Data Protection Commission is the regulator. Confidence: high.
- **Renewal copy.** 14-day withdrawal right for distance contracts (EU Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU, transposed by the EU (Consumer Information, Cancellation and Other Rights) Regulations 2013, now within the Consumer Rights Act 2022 framework). If the customer asks the service to start within the 14 days, they pay a proportionate amount, hence "minus any service already provided". Ireland has no general statutory auto-renewal reminder rule for digital subscriptions. Insurance has its own renewal-notice rules, which don't apply to us. The Consumer Rights Act 2022 lists automatic extension with an unreasonably early cancellation deadline as a potentially unfair term, so "cancel any time in Settings" keeps us safe. Watch the proposed EU Digital Fairness Act, which may add subscription-cancellation rules. Confidence: high.

## Salary (`sal.f = 0.2308`)
- Prior-knowledge ranges from Irish 2025-26 salary guides (Morgan McKinley Ireland Salary Guide, Hays Ireland, Robert Half Ireland, Barden, Lincoln Recruitment). For a Dublin industry role, Financial Analyst is about EUR 50-65k, Senior Financial Analyst or Senior FP&A Analyst about EUR 65-80k, and FP&A Manager about EUR 85-100k+. Base salary only, with bonus usually 5-15% on top. Chosen base is EUR 72k a year (EUR 6,000 a month), close to the senior midpoint. That is similar to the London pack's GBP 62.5k (about EUR 72k), which matches the usual Dublin/London parity for these roles. f = 6,000 / 26,000 = 0.2308. Expectation: 34,000 SAR x 0.2308 = about EUR 7,850 a month, or about EUR 94k a year. That is the top of the senior band and the start of FP&A Lead or Manager roles. Confidence: medium. **VERIFY** against the 2026 Morgan McKinley or Hays guide.

## Market facts used in copy
- **Cities** are ordered by finance/FP&A job density, from judgement: Dublin by far, then Cork (Apple, pharma, Musgrave), Limerick and Shannon (aircraft leasing, ADI, Northern Trust, Dell), Galway (medtech), Kilkenny (State Street, Glanbia), Waterford, then Athlone and the Midlands.
- **Employers** were chosen to cover the spec's sector list. Notes:
  - Banks: AIB, Bank of Ireland and PTSB are the three remaining retail banks after Ulster Bank and KBC left. No Islamic bank is relevant here.
  - Energy: ESB is the state-owned electricity champion.
  - Telecoms: eir is the incumbent and Vodafone the largest mobile operator. Which is "#1" depends on fixed vs mobile, and Three is close.
  - Public investment: NTMA runs ISIF, the sovereign development fund.
  - Exchange: Euronext Dublin is the former Irish Stock Exchange.
  - Industrial / mining: CRH, building materials and quarries. It moved its primary listing to the NYSE in 2023 and is still Irish-incorporated. Some head-office functions sit in the US, so **VERIFY** before calling it "Dublin HQ" in copy; the archetype avoids that phrase.
  - Hospital group: Bon Secours Health System is the largest private hospital group. I tagged it Cork (its largest hospital), but the group office may be in Dublin, so **VERIFY**.
  - Food delivery / e-commerce: Flipdish (Dublin-HQ ordering platform) was chosen over Deliveroo and Just Eat because those two run their finance functions outside Ireland.
  - AerCap's sector is the new value `leasing`, because no existing sector fits aircraft leasing. Map it to "Leasing" in the UI, or change it to `industrial`.
  - Logo colours are approximate brand colours, not official hex values.
- **Boards.** IrishJobs.ie and Jobs.ie are the two big domestic boards, alongside LinkedIn and Indeed. eFinancialCareers covers finance. publicjobs.ie is the central public-service recruiter, used by the Central Bank and NTMA among others.
- **Certs.** Ireland's four main accountancy bodies are Chartered Accountants Ireland (ACA), ACCA, CIMA and CPA Ireland. CFA was left out because qualified-accountant status is what Irish FP&A ads screen for. The persona holds the ACA, the most common Irish qualification.
- **govPlatforms: [].** Irish and EEA job seekers use no government platform. For permit cases the DETE Employment Permits Online System (EPOS) is used, but the employer or the applicant handles it after an offer. This is consistent with GB.
- **lang2: null.** Irish (Gaeilge) is only required for some public-sector and education roles, not finance.
- **Phone.** Irish mobiles are +353 8x xxx xxxx. ComReg does not reserve a fictional "drama" range that I know of, so the example uses the common placeholder +353 87 123 4567, the same style as the other EU packs.
- **Persona employer.** "Tolka Ridge Group / Tolka Ridge Logistics" is fictional, named after Dublin's River Tolka. I know of no large company with this name.
- **Culture.** The 39-hour standard week; the statutory minimum notice from an employee (1 week after 13 weeks' service, Minimum Notice and Terms of Employment Act 1973); the right to request remote work (Work Life Balance and Miscellaneous Provisions Act 2023, WRC Code of Practice 2024); auto-enrolment (My Future Fund) live from 1 Jan 2026; and PPS numbers for payroll. Confidence: high, except the auto-enrolment start date (medium-high).
